Derive the likelihood-ratio estimator

For a finite episodic objective J=Σ_tau P_theta(tau)G(tau), exchange sum and derivative under the required regularity. The identity ∇P=P∇log P gives ∇J=E[G∇log P]. Factor P as rho(s_0)Π_t π_theta(a_t|s_t)P(s_(t+1)|s_t,a_t). With theta-independent dynamics and initial distribution, ∇log P=Σ_t∇log π_theta(a_t|s_t). Unknown transition derivatives disappear, but the environment’s effects still determine sampled returns.

With discounted start objective G=Σ_k gamma^k r_k and return-to-go G_t=Σ_(k=t) gamma^(k−t)r_k, causality removes rewards before action t. Conditional on the history, that score has zero mean, so past rewards contribute zero in expectation. The exact episodic estimator is Σ_t gamma^t G_t∇log π_t. At gamma one the factor is one.

Some practical implementations average unweighted transition losses with discounted advantages. They use a related visitation/objective convention; do not silently identify that with the exact start-discounted episodic gradient above. State episode weighting, time weighting and batch reduction. Reward-to-go reduces irrelevant noise but does not guarantee a variance ordering for every finite estimator.

Finite support and stopping assumptions matter

Treat returns as sampled weights, detached from the policy graph. This derivation assumes allowed support and task dynamics are not changed by theta. Variable horizons and parameter-dependent stopping rules need careful formulation. A terminal episode’s full Monte Carlo return differs from bootstrapping at an external rollout cutoff.

Recap and self-check

Why does no model derivative not mean no model influence?

Dynamics shape trajectory probabilities and returns. Their theta-derivatives vanish under the assumption, while their sampled consequences remain in the estimator.
