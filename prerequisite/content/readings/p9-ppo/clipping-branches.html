Take the minimum after multiplying by advantage

The per-sample maximized objective is min(rho A,clip(rho,1−epsilon,1+epsilon)A). With epsilon .2, the interval is [.8,1.2]. Positive A=1 gives objectives .7,1,1.2,1.2 at ratios .7,1,1.3,1.8. Negative A=−1 gives −.8,−1,−1.3,−1.8 at the same ratios. The sign changes which branch is smaller.

For A>0, objective=A min(rho,1.2): derivative with respect to rho is A below 1.2, zero above. For A<0, objective=A max(rho,.8): derivative is zero below .8, A above. At exact thresholds the objective is nondifferentiable; framework subgradient conventions need not be used to infer a unique mathematical derivative.

The four-row pack witness uses ratios [1.3,.7,.7,1.3] and advantages [1,1,−1,−1]. Objectives [1.2,.7,−.8,−1.3] average −.05, so descent loss is +.05. Away from knots, descent gradients with respect to new log probabilities are [0,−.175,0,.325], because d rho/d logπ_new=rho and reduction divides by four.

Clip fraction counts thresholds, not active gradients

Every ratio in that witness is outside [.8,1.2], so clip fraction is one. Only two samples use flat branches. Therefore a ratio-outside diagnostic and a fraction-of-flat-objective diagnostic are different. Keep old logs and advantages detached while allowing the current log probability path.

def ppo_loss(new_log_probs, old_log_probs, advantage, epsilon=.2):
    r,lr,a=ratio_data(new_log_probs,old_log_probs,advantage,epsilon)
    return -torch.minimum(r*a,r.clamp(1-epsilon,1+epsilon)*a).mean()

Recap and self-check

Is the negative-advantage objective just minus the positive branch?

No. The minimum changes to the larger ratio under multiplication by a negative advantage.
