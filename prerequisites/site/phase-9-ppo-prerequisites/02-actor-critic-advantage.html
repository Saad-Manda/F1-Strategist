A baseline changes the noise, not the aim

Subtract any b(s) that doesn't depend on the call from G before scaling the log-gradient. The expected gradient doesn't move, because the extra term averages to zero:

Ea ~ π[ ∇log π(a | s) · b(s) ] = b(s) · ∇ Σa π(a | s) = b(s) · ∇1 = 0

The natural baseline is V(s), what the state is worth on average. It has to be learned alongside the policy, with Phase 4's machinery: that learned V is the critic, and the policy is the actor. What is left after subtracting it is the advantage:

A(s, a) = Q(s, a) − V(s) ∇J = Eπ[ ∇log π(a | s) · A(s, a) ]

Both returns are positive, so the raw update pushes both calls up, a2 by less. With the baseline, A = 8 − 5 = +3 for a1 and 2 − 5 = −3 for a2: a2 is pushed down. The advantage says which call was worse than the state's average, not only which was less good.

Page 01's raw reward is this picture with every return near −225: all pushes down. A baseline near −225 would turn them into ± a few seconds. The question is how soon the critic gets there.

A baseline that arrives late

The actor-critic below uses a table critic that moves 10% of the way to each return it sees. With the raw reward, by race 100 its value of lap 1 is already −254 to −261, close to the race's return. But by then the policy's calls have gone nearly deterministic: entropy 0.03 to 0.11 along its plan, against 0.66 at the start and 0.36 for the same learner centred. The first races' huge downward pushes have already settled it. It ends 23.49 to 33.94 s from the optimum, against 7.23 to 9.43 s centred.

Deep diveWhy the baseline term is zero, step by step110 words

∇log π · π = ∇π, the log-derivative identity of page 01 in reverse. So Σa π(a | s) ∇log π(a | s) · b(s) = b(s) Σa ∇π(a | s) = b(s) ∇ Σa π(a | s). The probabilities add up to 1 for every θ, so the gradient of the sum is 0. Nothing here needs b to be a good estimate: any b(s) keeps the gradient unbiased. A good one only makes it less noisy, and lab p9-02 measures both halves. Its part D adds a constant to every reward of a bandit, which is this phase's case in two arms.

Evidence so farA baseline turns all-negative pushes into ± a few seconds, but only once the critic has the scale. The table critic got there within 100 races, after the policy had stopped exploring: 23 to 34 s raw, 7 to 9 centred. Next: how PPO builds its advantages.
