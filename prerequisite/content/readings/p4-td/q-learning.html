A three-state world small enough to inspect

Use states s1, s2 and terminal s3. At s1, a0 gives −1 and reaches s2; a1 gives −3 and terminates. At s2, a0 terminates for −1 and a1 terminates for −2. The optimal path is a0 then a0, total −2. The other opening action totals −3.

Q-learning is given sampled transitions rather than these rules in advance. Its target is r+γ max_(legal a′) Q(s′,a′), removing continuation on true termination. Begin all cells at zero, use alpha 0.5 and gamma one, and process the original four samples in order:

An unvisited zero can be optimistic

The fourth sample still selects the untouched s2,a1 estimate of zero, even though its true value is −2. This does not establish that the implementation is wrong; it shows why unvisited entries and action coverage matter. The four updates do not reach the exact solution, and later noisy samples need not move every estimate monotonically toward it.

“Off-policy” here means the target's greedy continuation can differ from the behavior that generated experience. It does not mean illegal actions may enter the maximum, or that the learner can ignore the information rules of the task.

State convergence assumptions accurately

A standard tabular discounted convergence result assumes a finite stationary MDP, bounded rewards, sufficient state, consistent legal actions, infinite visits to relevant state/action pairs, and per-pair step sizes whose sum diverges while the sum of their squares converges. Alpha_n=1/n is an example of those step-size sums; finite practical runs cannot satisfy infinite visitation literally.

Fixed alpha in stochastic tasks generally keeps sample noise in the estimates; it does not guarantee convergence to Q* or even a universal fixed neighborhood. A deterministic special case may settle. Gamma-one episodic results need additional termination/properness assumptions rather than automatically following the discounted proof. The acyclic toy is safe to calculate at gamma one, but that does not prove every continuing gamma-one learner converges.

Reference: Watkins and Dayan's original Q-learning convergence paper.

Recap and self-check

Does running 10,000 episodes prove convergence?

No. It is a finite budget. Check visitation, representation, estimates and held-out performance; the theoretical conditions do not promise a practical convergence time.
