The one-paragraph restatement

Phase 6's Case 5: with position a sort of total times and nothing holding a car behind the one ahead, stopping before a rival gains nothing that being faster doesn't. Timing a stop against a rival's has no value of its own, and the rival's stop lap appears in none of your lap times.

What that means for self-play

Train 20,000 self-play races in such a race and there is no reward signal that could teach either learner that stopping before a rival has value of its own. The plan's headline Definition of Done, a two-humped histogram of pit deltas with peaks a lap or two before (undercut) and after (overcut) the rival's stop, can't appear, however well pages 01 to 05's machinery converges: the race has no mechanism such a histogram could be evidence of.

The numbers sayAll of them

With blocking off, lap 8 is each car's best stop against every lap the other could choose: the cars can't cost each other time, so each runs its own race. The equilibrium is (8, 8), and every pit delta is 0. With blocking on, 0.11 sits at 0, 0.61 on the follower's undercut side and 0.28 on the overcut side.

Across the 12 tyre settings tried for the capstone, blocking off gave a pure equilibrium with every delta 0 every time. Blocking is the whole difference. Below, the duel with blocking off: every leader rule plays lap 8, and nothing you pick beats −1.025.

Which file the rule goes in: not only Phase 6's

The plan's F1PettingZooEnv does not use Phase 6's F1MultiCarEnv. It builds one independent single-car F1StrategyEnv per car, steps each on its own, adds up each car's own lap times and sorts the totals. No car's lap time ever depends on another's: attempt_overtake is imported and never called, and dirty air is subtracted from the reward but not the lap time (audit E3, again). Adding the rule to F1MultiCarEnv is right for Phase 6 and changes nothing here. For Phase 8 it has to go into F1PettingZooEnv.step, or the env has to be rebuilt on F1MultiCarEnv.

Two tempting fixes that miss

The plan's Risks table now says the right thing: its "Undercut doesn't emerge" row says to check the blocking model first, not the out-lap warm-up. Two fixes are still tempting when the histogram stays flat. More frequent checkpoints and better recency tuning (page 03) address non-stationarity: they change how good the opponents are, not what the race lets anyone learn. Tuning the warm-up changes how big an undercut would be, not whether one can exist.
