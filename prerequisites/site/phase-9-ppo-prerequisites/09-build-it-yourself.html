Build It Yourself: Policy Gradient on the Toy Race

Seven stages on the 20-lap toy race of the earlier capstones: a table policy over the legal actions, REINFORCE, GAE and a critic, one destructive update, the clip that stops it, a small PPO, and what the clip is really for when each batch is reused ten times.

This page is the brief for the capstone lab below. The world is the one the Phase 3.5, 4 and 5 capstones used: the quiet toy race (no safety car, dry), three actions (stay out, pit onto A, pit onto B), and a ruler that evaluates a greedy policy exactly and subtracts the optimum, 224.49 s. A gap of 0.00 means optimal; a uniformly random legal policy is 43.45 s away. The reward of a lap is −(lap time − 10), centred, for the reason page 02 and lab p9-02 part D give. Each stage is checked on its own, and your learners must reproduce the reference's numbers seed for seed, so the given loops call your functions in a fixed order.

Stage 1 — a policy that only puts weight on legal actions

The policy is a table: one row of three logits per state (lap, compound, tyre age, used both compounds). The probabilities are a softmax over the legal actions only, which is what MaskablePPO does with its −1e8 logits (page 06). Write masked_probs and log_grad, page 01's log-gradient with zeros on the illegal actions. The check compares it with finite differences.

Reference snippet (a last-resort hint: try the stage first) 
def masked_probs(logits, mask):
    z = np.where(mask, logits, -np.inf)
    z = z - z[mask].max()
    p = np.exp(z)
    return p / p.sum()

def log_grad(logits, mask, action):
    g = -masked_probs(logits, mask)
    g[action] += 1.0
    return np.where(mask, g, 0.0)

Stage 2 — REINFORCE, and a sign error that is loud

Page 01's update, lap by lap: θ[s_t] += α · G_t · ∇log π(a_t | s_t), with G_t the return from lap t to the flag. After 2,000 races it should be well clear of a random policy, and with the sign flipped clearly worse. That second check is the point: a sign error is the kind of bug the learning curve shows you.

Reference snippet (a last-resort hint: try the stage first) 
G = returns_to_go(race.rewards)
for key, mask, action, g in zip(race.keys, race.masks, race.actions, G):
    theta[key] = theta[key] + sign * alpha * g * log_grad(theta[key], mask, action)

Measured in the build: REINFORCE stalls between 3.5 and 13 s from the optimum, and more races do not rescue it. Its choices stop being random before it has found the good pit lap. The report shows this as the policy's entropy falling to zero.

Stage 3 — GAE and a critic

Page 03's backward recursion for one complete race: δ_t = r_t + γV(s_t+1) − V(s_t), with the value after the last lap 0, and A_t = δ_t + γλ · A_t+1. The check uses page 03's own numbers. The critic is a table too: each update moves V[s] a fraction of the way towards its target.

Reference snippet (a last-resort hint: try the stage first) 
values = list(values) + [0.0]
adv, run = np.zeros(len(rewards)), 0.0
for t in reversed(range(len(rewards))):
    delta = rewards[t] + gamma * values[t + 1] - values[t]
    run = delta + gamma * lam * run
    adv[t] = run

Stage 4 — one destructive update

Page 04's motivating problem, on purpose. Start from a good PPO table (1.93 s from the optimum after 1,000 races), take one race, and on each of its laps take one large plain policy-gradient step towards the action the policy liked least. Measured: the gap goes from 1.93 to 21.28 s. One step on one race undid a thousand races of learning.

Stage 5 — the clip, and what it does not do

Write one step on page 04's clipped objective for one sample. Its gradient is ratio · A · ∇log π while the unclipped term is the smaller one, and exactly zero once the clip binds: A > 0 and the ratio at or above 1 + ε, or A < 0 and the ratio at or below 1 − ε. Then repeat stage 4's scenario with twenty clipped steps per lap instead of one plain one. The policy is left where it was. But look at the largest ratio reached: measured 4.95, far past 1.2. The clip stops the pushing; it does not undo the step that crossed the line. Lab p9-04 part 3 works the same overshoot by hand.

Reference snippet (a last-resort hint: try the stage first) 
ratio = masked_probs(logits, mask)[action] / p_old

Stage 6 — a small PPO

Collect ten races, compute GAE advantages with the critic, standardise them across the batch (one of page 05's details that matter), then make a few passes over the batch in a shuffled order, a clipped step and a critic update for each sample. The probability of each action under the policy that ran the race is frozen at collection time: recomputing it each epoch is one of page 05's quiet bugs (lab p9-05). Measured: 1.93 s from the optimum after 2,000 races, about eight seconds a run.

Stage 7 — ten epochs per batch

No new code: run your PPO with epochs=10, the real config's n_epochs, with and without the clip. At four epochs the two are close (after 2,000 races, 1.93 and 2.96 s on seeds 0 and 1 without the clip, 1.93 on both with it). At ten they are not: after 1,000 races, 1.71 s with the clip and 7.19 s without on seed 0, and over four seeds 1.67 to 2.37 s against 2.81 to 10.65 s. Reusing each batch many times is what PPO is for, and the clip is what makes it safe.

The report

The lab's report notebook, about six minutes of given cells, puts the four learners side by side with their entropy, plots the largest ratio in every update at ten epochs (70% of updates pass 1.2, the largest 2.61), and trains Stable-Baselines3's MaskablePPO on the same race twice. With the reward centred it finds the exact optimum (0.00 s) in 1,000 races. With the raw reward, −lap time, it learns nothing measurable (38.48 s, the gap of an untrained network). That is audit finding J3, measured.
