Phase 8 · Page 05 · The first opponent

Why the Pool's First Member Matters

"ε = 0" and "a random opponent" sound alike. They aren't, and mixing them up shapes the first thousand races of training.

Your call · an untrained opponent

Thirty freshly initialised copies of the plan's network, DuelingDQN(19, 6, 128), each played greedily (ε = 0) through one 52-lap race, with the lap and tyre age moving.

How many of the thirty pick the same action on every lap?

What the plan does

train_self_play seeds the pool before training with pool.add(learner, episode=0): a freshly initialised, untrained network. pool.sample() then sets epsilon = 0.0 on whatever it returns, this first entry included.

Untrained is not random

A greedy policy over randomly initialised weights isn't noisy or varied. It is a deterministic function of those fixed weights: the same observation gives the same action every time, for as long as the checkpoint is in the pool. "Untrained" says how good a policy is. It says nothing about how varied it is, and the two are easy to conflate.

The reveal · an untrained opponent

You calledno call made
The numbers say23 of 30

23 of the 30 pick the same action on every lap. Over 2,000 random observations the most common greedy action takes a median 95% of them (the lowest, 51%). The action entropy of an untrained greedy network is 0.20 nats, against 1.79 for a uniformly random policy (lab p8-05).

The plan's step replaces an illegal action with the first legal one, so a network stuck on “pit onto the compound I already have” alternates between two behaviours rather than pitting 52 times. It is still a fixed, exploitable script.

Why it matters, and why it comes second

The danger isn't that the first opponent is weak. A weak but varied opponent is a fine start. The danger is that it has no variance: every race before the next checkpoint arrives, up to 1,000 of them, is played against the same fixed answer, and the learner is trained to exploit one quirk of one random initialisation. But with the plan as written, this never shows up. The pool's sample() fails first (page 03). Fix the crash, then the seed.

The fix

  • Seed with a stochastic policy: epsilon = 1.0, uniformly random calls, for the pool's earliest entries. Variety at once, at the cost of an opponent that is unskilled throughout.
  • Seed with Phase 3's rule-based agents, the plan's stronger suggestion: competent and diverse at once, a curriculum from episode 0.
Deep diveThe same trap elsewhere60 words

"A greedy policy over an untrained function is not a random baseline" reaches well past self-play. Anywhere code uses "hasn't learned yet" for "behaves like noise", such as an untrained classifier as a control group or an unfitted model as a null, the same gap can hide: the thing meant to supply variety may be one silently repeated answer.
