A race, written down in five parts

The loop on page 01 needs a precise version before anything can be computed from it. That version is a Markov decision process, or MDP: five things, written (S, A, P, R, γ). Here they are read off the Pit Wall's panel.

PartWhat it isOn the Pit Wall
S, statesevery situation you could be in(lap, tyre, tyre age, safety car, both used, wet): 3,360 of them
A, actionsthe choices, some ruled out by a maskSTAY OUT, BOX FOR A, BOX FOR B
P, transitionsP(s′ | s, a): how likely each next state is, given this state and callthe tyre ages a lap or resets; the safety car arrives with probability 6%
R, rewardone number for each transitionminus the lap time
γ, discounthow much a later reward counts, between 0 and 11: every lap counts the same

The real project has the same five parts, each with a named counterpart in dynamics.py or f1_strategy_env.py: 16-number observations, six pit choices, compute_lap_time plus the bookkeeping in step(), -lap_time, and γ = 1.0. That is what "Phase 1 defines the MDP" means. Page 06 goes through them one by one.

The state must carry the whole past that matters

The M in MDP is the Markov property: what happens next depends on the current state and action only, not on how you got there. It is not automatically true. It is a demand on whoever designs the state. If two histories lead to the same state and then behave differently, the state is missing something.

The Pit Wall's state carries a flag for this reason: both used. A car that has never run B must stop by lap 19 in a dry race; a car that has can stay out to the flag. Without the flag, the past would decide the future and the state would not show it.

Past lap times say nothing the tyre age does not. On this track a tyre's wear is a function of its compound and age, so the age is the whole story. That is the Markov property doing its job.

Phase 1 derives the out-lap warm-up from tyre_age and rejects a second laps_since_pit counter. tyre_age == 1 already means "out-lap", so the second counter would be redundant. Worse, two counters that can drift out of step are a way for hidden history to slip back into the transition function without anyone noticing. One variable, one meaning.

What a policy is

A policy π maps each state to an action, or to probabilities over actions. Solving an MDP means finding the policy with the most total reward. Phase 1 builds the world the policy acts in and no policy at all. Every later phase is a different way to represent and improve one for this same MDP: rules of thumb in Phase 3, a table in Phase 4, a neural network in Phase 5.

Why every lap counts the same

The return is what the agent maximises: the sum of the rewards still to come, each one weighted by γ once more than the one before it.

G_t = R_(t+1) + γ·R_(t+2) + γ²·R_(t+3) + γ³·R_(t+4) + ...

With γ = 0 the agent only sees the next reward. With γ close to 1, distant rewards count almost as much as near ones. Phase 1 uses γ = 1.0, so the return is the plain sum of -lap_time: minus the race time. Any γ below 1 makes late laps count for less than early ones (the lab measures by how much). That rewards pace early in the race at the cost of the finish, which no race does. The plan's comment says it in one line: "γ = 1.0 (fixed-length episodes; avoids late-race pace bias)".

Deep diveIs γ = 1 safe? The sum could be infinite60 words

In a task that never ends, a γ below 1 is what keeps the return finite. A race ends: every episode is total_laps long, so the sum has a fixed number of terms and is finite whatever γ is. With that worry gone, γ = 1 is the one value that matches the goal, the total race time.

Who rules out an illegal call?

You could give every state all actions and punish the bad ones, say with a reward of −∞, and let the agent learn to avoid them. When you know in advance which actions are illegal, it is faster and safer to remove them. That is an action mask: next to each state, a list of true/false values saying which actions are legal now. On the Pit Wall it is the greyed-out button and its reason.

Deep diveThe project's mask rules, and why they live in one function90 words

The mask enforces the regulations: intermediates are illegal in the dry, slicks are illegal in the wet, you cannot pit for the compound you are on, and the two-dry-compound rule forces a stop once it is the only way left to meet it. The environment and the Phase 3.5 oracle must agree on what is legal. So the plan writes the mask once, as a shared free function, compute_action_mask in dynamics.py, that both call. One place to get the rule wrong, not two places that can quietly disagree.

STAY_OUT. Age 14 is below MEDIUM's cliff at 18, so wear is linear: 0.030 × 14 = 0.420 s. With 22 laps to go, the fuel costs (22 × 1.600) × 0.033 = 1.162 s. No weather penalty, no warm-up. Called with rng=None, the expected-value form the oracle uses, there is no noise term:

lap_time = 92.000 + 0.000 + 0.420 + 1.162 + 0.000 + 0.000 = 93.582 s reward = −93.582

Next state: lap 31, MEDIUM, age 15.

PIT_HARD. The plan's ordering rule: the pit lap is still driven on the old tyre, so the lap itself is the same 93.582 s. The pit loss is added on top, in full, since there is no safety car:

lap_time = 93.582 + pit_loss(GREEN) = 93.582 + 22.000 = 115.582 s reward = −115.582

Next state: lap 31, HARD, age 1 (the next lap is the out-lap, so its warm-up applies then), and both MEDIUM and HARD used, which settles the two-compound rule.

On the rewards alone, −93.582 beats −115.582 and staying out wins. It always will on a single lap: a stop costs 22 s now, and fresh tyres only pay back on later laps. The comparison that decides is between the two returns, the rest of the race after each call. That is page 03.

Worked solution

1. γ = 0: −2. γ = 0.5: −2 + 0.5(−1) + 0.25(−3) + 0.125(−1) + 0.0625(−4) = −3.625. γ = 1: −11.

2. Weight on the last five of twenty steps is (γ¹⁵ − γ²⁰) / (1 − γ²⁰). At γ = 0.9 that is 0.0843 / 0.8784 ≈ 9.6%. At γ = 1 all weights are equal: 5 / 20 = 25%.

3. Degradation 0.020 × 20 = 0.400. Fuel (12 × 1.600) × 0.033 = 0.634. STAY_OUT: 92.000 + 0.550 + 0.400 + 0.634 = 93.584 s. PIT_MEDIUM: 93.584 + 22.000 = 115.584 s.

4. The static maze and the tyre state are Markov. The door maze hides whether the key was collected; the last lap time alone hides the tyre's age and compound.
