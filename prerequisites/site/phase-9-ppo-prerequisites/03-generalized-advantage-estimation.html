The advantage Stable-Baselines3 computes: a dial between one-step and whole-race estimates, built from the critic's residuals.

Rewards −3, −2, −3, −2. The critic's values for the four states: −10, −6, −5, −1, and 0 after the flag. γ = 1. With λ = 1, GAE gives the whole-race advantage, G0 − V(s0).

What is A0 at λ = 1?

Two estimates you already know

The one-step TD residual is itself an advantage estimate: low noise, biased whenever V is wrong. The whole-race Gt − V(st) is unbiased but noisy, page 01's problem.

δt = rt + γ V(st+1) − V(st) AtGAE(λ) = Σl ≥ 0 (γλ)l δt+l

λ is the dial: at 0 only δt is left; at 1 the sum telescopes to Gt − V(st). It is TD(λ)'s idea applied to the advantage. Computed backwards, it is one line: At = δt + γλ · At+1.

For the race above, δ = (1, −1, 1, −1). At λ = 0, A0 = 1. At λ = 0.95, the value Stable-Baselines3 uses by default, A0 = 1 − 0.95 + 0.9025 − 0.857 ≈ 0.095.

At λ = 1, A0 = 1 − 1 + 1 − 1 = 0, and G0 − V(s0) = −10 − (−10) = 0: this race came in on what the critic expected. The three answers, 1, about 0.1 and 0, come from the same four residuals weighted differently.

Every residual is made of the critic's values. While V is far from the truth, δ mostly measures the critic's error, the same whichever call was made. GAE is only as good as its critic.

A PPO that learns from the raw reward

Pages 03 and 04 together are the mini PPO of this phase's labs: GAE advantages from a table critic, batches of 10 races, advantages standardised per batch, a few clipped passes over each. With the raw reward it learns: 1.71, 4.35 and 4.25 s from the optimum after 2,000 races at 4 passes per batch, and 0.88, 5.21 and 1.71 at 10, against 1.43 to 1.93 centred. Its critic moves 10% of its error per visit, so it reaches −242 to −254 within 100 races, and standardising the advantages keeps every step a sensible size while it does. Its entropy at race 100 is still 0.36 to 0.56; the actor-critic's was 0.03 to 0.11.

Deep diveWhy λ = 1 telescopes100 words

With γ = 1, Σl δt+l = Σl (rt+l + V(st+l+1) − V(st+l)). Each V(st+l+1) cancels the −V of the next term, leaving Σ rt+l + V(send) − V(st) = Gt − V(st), since the value after the flag is 0. The same cancellation with γ < 1 gives the discounted return. Lab p9-03 builds this over a buffer holding several races, where an episode end has to cut the backward carry, and checks the result against Stable-Baselines3's own buffer.

Evidence so farPPO's own machinery copes with the raw reward when its critic can keep up: the mini PPO, with a table critic, ends 0.88 to 5.21 s raw. So “PPO can't learn from a raw reward” is not the answer. Next: the clip.

Hint 1

next_values has four cases; get the ending ones first. A terminated step bootstraps from 0, a truncated one from final_values[t]. Otherwise the next step's value, unless the buffer has run out, which is what last_value is for. The backward pass carries one number from the end towards the start, and an episode end cuts the carry.

Hint 2

td_residuals is one numpy line: rewards + gamma * next_vals - values. In gae: if ends[t]: carry = 0.0; then carry = deltas[t] + gamma * lam * carry; adv[t] = carry.

Hint 3

If only test_agrees_with_stable_baselines3 fails: SB3 marks the START of each episode (episode_starts), you mark the END. A step t is followed by a new episode exactly when ends[t] is True, which is how the test builds SB3's buffer.
