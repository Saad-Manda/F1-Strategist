Change of sampling changes the average being optimized

Suppose the desired replay objective is the uniform mean (1/N)Σ_i loss_i, but index i is drawn with probability P(i). At beta one, the unnormalized correction w_i=1/(N P(i)) gives Σ_i P(i) w_i loss_i=(1/N)Σ_i loss_i. This finite-sum identity explains importance weighting. It assumes every desired item has positive probability and weights correspond to the actual sampling law.

For beta between zero and one, w_i=(N P(i))^(−β) partially corrects the sampling shift; beta zero gives ones. Increasing beta trades a changed objective against variability from large corrections. It is not an automatic guarantee that an approximate neural learner becomes unbiased.

Keep the normalization convention explicit

The pack divides weights by the largest weight over the whole retained buffer. With N=3 and P=[1/6,1/3,1/2], beta one gives raw weights [2,1,2/3], normalized [1,1/2,1/3]. With squared errors [1,4,9], weighted_loss on this supplied three-item list is (1+2+3)/3=2.

That list is a chosen witness, not a randomly sampled batch from P. For actual draws, expected normalized weighted loss at beta one is the uniform mean (14/3) divided by 2, hence 7/3. Whole-buffer maximum normalization multiplies the unbiased correction by a known common factor; it changes its scale. It does not change the ideal weighted relative contributions at fixed probabilities.

Normalizing by the largest weight in only the realized minibatch gives a random factor depending on which entries appeared. It generally cannot be called exactly unbiased. If a sampled batch contains only item 2, its pack-global normalized weight remains 1/3, whereas minibatch normalization makes it 1. The project plan and pack differ here; label the convention instead of mixing their numbers.

Reduce after applying per-item weights

def weighted_loss(losses, weights):
    losses = np.asarray(losses,dtype=float)
    weights = np.asarray(weights,dtype=float)
    if (losses.ndim != 1 or not len(losses) or weights.shape != losses.shape
        or not np.isfinite(losses).all() or not np.isfinite(weights).all()
        or (losses < 0).any() or (weights < 0).any() or (weights > 1).any()):
        raise ValueError("invalid losses/weights")
    return float(np.mean(weights*losses))

The denominator is batch count, not sum(weights). A weight tensor and per-item loss must both be [B], avoiding accidental [B,B] broadcasting. Keep weights detached: they determine the sampling correction for this update rather than supplying a new trainable loss path. dqn_step already accepts this [B] vector.
