No unilateral improvement at a Nash equilibrium

A pair (p*,q*) is Nash if neither player improves its own expected payoff by changing only its strategy while the other is fixed. In this zero-sum matrix game, uniform q makes every learner pure action score zero. Uniform p similarly leaves every opponent deviation at zero. Thus uniform/uniform is a mixed Nash equilibrium.

A best-response calculation forms v=Mq and chooses every maximum-valued row. Against q=(.5,.25,.25), v=(0,.25,−.25) so PAPER is best. Against uniform q all three are best. The learner’s exploitability diagnostic in this zero-sum toy is the best opponent’s benefit: −min_j(pᵀM)_j; pure PAPER has 1 and uniform has 0.

Equilibrium is a joint condition, not merely a high self-play score. A current checkpoint beating its previous version need not be closer to equilibrium. Nor does a finite training history prove absence of profitable deviations outside the tested opponent set.

Keep existence and convergence separate

The mixed equilibrium can be verified directly from finite payoffs. A simultaneous latest-pure best-response procedure can cycle instead of settling. Statements about fictitious-play convergence concern particular games and empirical mixtures under their update assumptions. A recency-biased finite neural checkpoint pool does not inherit those guarantees.

def best_response(opponent):
    values = RPS @ policy_vector(opponent)
    return [int(i) for i in np.flatnonzero(np.isclose(values,values.max(),rtol=0,atol=1e-12))]

Recap and self-check

Does playing uniform guarantee a positive win margin?

No. In this toy it secures expected payoff zero; it does not guarantee every finite sampled match ties.
