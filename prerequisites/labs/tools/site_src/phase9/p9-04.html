Move the ratio, the advantage and ε, and watch where the objective stops rewarding a push. Then fill in the clip table for new numbers, find the cells where the gradient is zero, and work out by hand how far one step can carry a ratio past the clip.

1 · The objective, live

The solid line is min(r·A, clip(r, 1−ε, 1+ε)·A); the dashed line is r·A on its own. In the shaded region the solid line is flat, so its slope in r, and with it the gradient the update follows, is zero.

2 · The clip table for ε = 0.1

With ε = 0.1 the band is [0.9, 1.1]. Enter min(r·A, clip(r)·A) for each cell.

In which cells is the gradient with respect to r zero? Tick all that apply.

3 · One step can overshoot

Two actions. The taken one had p_old = 0.1 and advantage A = 5; ε = 0.2. Start at r = 1 (the policy has not moved yet) and take one gradient step of size 0.1 on the logits. At r = 1 the gradient of r·A with respect to the taken action's logit is A · (1 − p) = 5 × 0.9, and the other logit's is the negative of that.

Does the clip pull the ratio back to 1.2 on the next step?

4 · Reading it off the widget

With A < 0, for which ratios does the objective still reward moving r, and why is that safe?
Which SB3 training logs would show that ratios are overshooting? Tick all that apply.
Worked solution

The table for ε = 0.1: A = +2 gives 1.7, 2, 2.2, 2.2; A = −2 gives −1.8, −2, −2.3, −3.2. The gradient is zero in (c), (d) and (e). In (a) and (h) r is outside the band, but the unclipped term is smaller and the push is back towards the band.

Overshoot: each logit moves by 0.45, the difference by 0.9, p goes from 0.1 to 0.2146, and the ratio from 1 to 2.15 in one step. The clip does not undo it; it only stops further pushing. clip_fraction and approx_kl show it happening.
