Page 08 / 12 — An audit finding, taught as a core lesson (J2)

"Same Timesteps" Isn't "Same Experience"

A stated fairness protocol, and the arithmetic that quietly contradicts it.

The stated protocol

The real plan's fair-comparison protocol states, as item 4 of 5: "Same total environment steps — ~2M steps for both." This is exactly the right instinct — Phase 3's paired-seed evaluation discipline (assumed background) only controls for luck in which scenarios got sampled during evaluation; it says nothing about whether the two agents being compared were actually given the same amount of training experience to work with.

The arithmetic that contradicts it

DQN (Phase 5): 5,000 episodes × ~52 laps/episode ≈ 260,000 environment steps PPO (this phase): configs/ppo.yaml sets total_timesteps: 2,000,000 2,000,000 / 260,000 ≈ 7.7

PPO receives roughly eight times the environment interaction the stated protocol claims to be controlling for.

A compounding error, worth naming precisely

The same config file's own comment describes its total_timesteps: 2000000 as "~2M steps; equivalent to ~5000 episodes" — but 2,000,000 / 52 ≈ 38,500 episodes, not 5,000. The config's own self-description is off by roughly the same 7.7× factor as the actual DQN/PPO mismatch, which is itself informative: it suggests this wasn't a deliberate choice to give PPO more data, but two numbers that were meant to agree and quietly didn't.

Why this specifically undermines the phase's headline claim

Any result of the form "PPO reached a better (or worse) race time than DQN" is uninterpretable once one algorithm has had 7.7× the training data. The difference could reflect the algorithm, the sample budget, or some mix of both, and the real plan's Definition of Done as written — "DQN vs PPO comparison runs on 100 paired seeds" — controls only for evaluation-time luck, not for this training-time confound. A comparison can be statistically rigorous at evaluation time and still be a comparison of the wrong thing.

Two genuine fixes

Not "just increase DQN's budget to match" in isolation — that would silently change a separately-tuned hyperparameter (Phase 5's 5,000-episode budget) without stating that's what happened. Two options that are actually principled:

  1. Match the budgets explicitly before training either agent, so the protocol's own stated intent — "same total environment steps" — is genuinely met, and say in the write-up what number was chosen and why.
  2. Train both to their own tuned budget, but report learning curves on a shared environment-step x-axis — which the comparison notebook's own Cell 3 already intends to do ("Align x-axis on 'environment steps' for fair comparison"), but which the current configs actively work against. This option is arguably the better one: sample efficiency is itself a genuine, reportable difference between an off-policy method (DQN) and an on-policy one (PPO), so making the training-step gap a visible, labeled part of the result turns a hidden confound into a real finding, rather than papering over it.
Tied to Phase 3's evaluation discipline, not a separate concern

Controlling the evaluation seeds carefully — Phase 3's paired-seed framework, assumed background — means very little if the training budgets that produced the two agents being compared were never matched, or at least never reported. A rigorous evaluation protocol wrapped around an unmatched training setup is still an unfair comparison; the rigor at the end doesn't retroactively fix the confound built in at the start.
