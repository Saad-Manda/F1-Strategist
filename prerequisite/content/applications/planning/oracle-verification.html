A solved table needs a tested policy adapter

The project plan separates src/evaluation/dp_oracle.py, src/env/weather_markov.py and scripts/run_dp_oracle.py. These are planned responsibilities: the solver enumerates/backs up states, the weather module fits/samples conditional rows, and the runner evaluates a policy. Read the plan after completing the toy work; the course checkpoint acknowledges preparation, not production correctness.

A policy adapter must map the available observation and current action mask to a supported solver state and action. It cannot reconstruct hidden future weather from a private simulator handle. Keep exact internal state access, privileged Oracle A information and ordinary Oracle B observation access explicitly separated. Test whether decoding normalized or clipped observations loses the distinctions the table needs.

Verify from several directions

  1. On the deterministic four-lap model, reproduce cost 52, pit at lap two, final STAY and documented ties. Compare against direct enumeration and rollout.
  2. At horizons zero/one, ensure terminal zero and immediate cost once. A nonterminal empty legal set must produce a declared error, not an invented zero.
  3. For two-step stochastic cases, enumerate action choices at each observed successor and their probabilities independently. Open-loop action sequences alone omit adaptive policies.
  4. With mandatory service, check final unserved PIT and feasible policy rollout; restrict actions without changing costs or random laws before testing that minimum cost cannot improve.
  5. Validate all weather/SC rows, supplied draw boundaries, impossible events, seed reproducibility, per-row sample counts and held-out fit.
  6. Verify a physical current-lap trace, SC/VSC pit costs, old/new tyre ordering, usage bits and the shared mask. Check pure queries do not mutate inputs or consume the live stream.
  7. For conditional additive zero-mean noise, compare simulated means with the analytic value; separately test any clipping or noise-dependent state effects.
  8. For aligned information sets and distributions, check A's expected cost bound with the correct sign; keep template and Markov-mode results distinct.

Use a baseline as a diagnostic, with the same task

On the deterministic toy, never pitting costs 58 and a fixed lap-two pit costs 52. DP is at most the cost of any feasible policy in that same model. For stochastic models, evaluate a fixed-lap policy under the same SC/weather law rather than comparing a stochastic DP value with a noise-free dry baseline. A surprisingly large benefit deserves physical inspection, but an arbitrary “within 10%” bound is not a universal correctness theorem.

Reusing dynamics prevents implementation drift. Independent arithmetic and model invariants catch shared mistakes, such as a negative pit loss or free SC lap. Agreement between two callers of the same broken function cannot prove physical correctness.

Evaluate the policy, not just its initial table cell

The table's initial value predicts expected cost under the specified model. Roll the extracted policy through the environment with its normal randomness enabled. Compare independent race means and uncertainty against that prediction, logging settings and model versions. A mismatch can arise from a solver bug, a policy decoder bug, timing drift, different random laws or sampling variation.

For paired agent/oracle results, compute each seed's time difference and bootstrap whole paired races if that inference method is chosen. Do not resample individual correlated laps as independent races. Retain physical lap-time diagnostics, violation counts and model-fit evidence alongside the headline gap.

Recap and self-check

Is a low C(start) sufficient evidence that the deployed adapter performs well?

No. The adapter may decode differently, leak information or select an illegal action. A rollout check exercises the complete interface, while independent witnesses anchor its model.
