Expectations Over Randomness

The oracle cannot know whether the safety car comes. It can know how likely it is. This page is the case's answer.

Your call · race #257, lap 8

The oracle boxed for B under green, for 4 s. On lap 10 the safety car came out, when a stop costs 1.6 s. You waited, boxed under it, and finished 2.36 s ahead.

Was the oracle's lap-8 stop a mistake?

What does "on average" mean on lap 8?

From a green lap 8, every call leads to two possible lap 9s: still green, or a safety car. The oracle cannot know which. So it weighs them by their odds, 94% and 6%, and the value of the next lap becomes a weighted average of two numbers the backward pass already has:

E[ V_9 ] = 0.94 · V_9(green) + 0.06 · V_9(safety car)

Work it for both calls on lap 8 of race #257:

stay out: 11.36 + 0.94 × 141.79 + 0.06 × 145.08 = 153.34 box for B: 15.36 + 0.94 × 136.93 + 0.06 × 143.16 = 152.66

Boxing wins by 0.68 s. A safety car on lap 9 makes the rest of the race slower whatever you are on, but the stop still pays. This is the only new piece of maths in the phase. Wherever the backward pass used "the" next value, it now uses the average over every next situation that can happen, weighted by its chance. Everything else stays the same.

Where do the odds come from?

A hazard model: each green lap is a small, independent chance of a safety car, fitted per circuit from real races in Phase 0.5. Once out, each lap is a chance for it to come in.

P(safety car next lap | green now) = hazard P(safety car next lap | out now) = 1 − 1 / mean_duration

On the Pit Wall the hazard is 6% and the safety car stays out with probability 2/3, 3 laps on average. The hazard is fitted per circuit because real circuits differ widely: a street circuit like Singapore has seen a safety car in nearly every race, Bahrain in few. One number for all of them would put the oracle's pit windows wrong at both ends.

So how did you beat it?

Any race time splits into three parts. Start from the expected race time, V at the start: 231.92 s. Each call adds its cost: how much slower the rest of the race is, on average, after it than after the best call. That is never negative. Each lap's dice add or take away luck, with either sign. The three always add up to the race time.

Your calls cost 2.47 s: staying out on lap 8 (0.68 s) and again on lap 9 (1.78 s), each rounded. Then the safety car came as your A tyres gave out, and handed you a 1.6 s stop: 6.63 s of good luck. The oracle's luck was 1.80 s. Yours beat its by 4.83 s, more than your calls cost. Better dice, not better calls.

On other dice it goes the other way. Over 20,000 races, waiting for the safety car beats the oracle in 2,436 (12%) and is 6.23 s slower on average: 238.15 against 231.92. The oracle is the best on average. No strategy that cannot see the future is the best on every set of dice.

Deep diveWhy the split always adds up160 words

Write the race as laps 1 to 20 with situations s_1, s_2, … and lap times c_t, and set V after the flag to 0. The sum of V(s_t+1) − V(s_t) over the race telescopes to −V(s_1), so

time − V(s_1) = Σ_t [ c_t + V(s_t+1) − V(s_t) ] = Σ_t [ Q(s_t, a_t) − V(s_t) ] + Σ_t [ c_t + V(s_t+1) − Q(s_t, a_t) ] = cost of the calls + luck

Q(s, a) is the expected time to the flag after call a, and V(s) is the smallest Q, so each cost term is zero or more. Each luck term is the next situation's value minus what it was expected to be, so it averages to zero. The oracle's calls cost nothing, which is why its time differs from 231.92 only by luck. Your split on the Pit Wall is this sum, lap by lap: open This race as an MDP after the flag.

Deep diveWhy the safety-car discount is a cost, not a reward100 words

The plan has a stop under the safety car cost about 40% of the usual pit loss, because the whole field is running slowly. That belongs in cost(s, a), the immediate term, computed by dynamics.compute_pit_loss. It has nothing to do with the expectation over next situations. An easy mistake is to zero the reward on safety-car laps instead. That also zeroes the pit loss, makes a safety-car stop free rather than cheap, and the best policy collapses into "always wait for a safety car", a strategy that only looks good because of a bookkeeping error.

Deep diveWhy compute_lap_time with no noise is exact120 words

The backward pass needs one number for cost(s, a), not a distribution. The live environment adds random noise to every lap. The oracle may drop it, and the reason is two lines, not a shortcut:

lap_time = deterministic_part(state, action) + noise, E[noise] = 0 E[lap_time] = deterministic_part + E[noise] = deterministic_part = compute_lap_time(..., noise=0)

Because the noise is added on and averages to zero, dropping it gives the expected lap time itself, not an approximation. That is why the plan has the oracle call dynamics.compute_lap_time with noise off rather than restating the formula: a second copy would have to prove the same property for its own noise, and two copies that drift apart are a bug that looks right in each file.

Evidence so farThe case's maths is closed. The oracle is the best on average; on race #257 your calls cost 2.47 s and your luck beat the oracle's by 4.83 s. Next: an oracle that knows more than this one.
