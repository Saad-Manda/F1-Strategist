Differentiate a selected log probability

Write log π_a=z_a−log Σ_j exp(z_j). Its derivative with respect to z_k is 1[k=a]−π_k: the one-hot selected action minus the probability vector. For logits [1,0] and action 0 this is [.26894142,−.26894142]. The two components sum to zero because adding a common logit offset leaves the policy unchanged.

For each action form its score vector e_a−π. Averaging under π gives Σ_a π_a(e_a−π)=π−πΣ_a π_a=0. This zero-mean identity is central to removing past rewards and action-independent baselines later. It is an expectation under the same policy, not the claim that an individual sampled score is zero.

Logit gradients become parameter gradients through the chain rule: ∇_theta log π_a=Σ_k(1[k=a]−π_k)∇_theta z_k. Masked finite legal logits follow the same identity over the fixed legal support. Illegal logits have no score contribution.

State the assumptions before using the identity

Policy probabilities must be differentiable on allowed support, normalized, and positive for sampled actions. The environment and initial-state distribution must not have unaccounted dependence on policy parameters. If a learned mask or changing support depends on theta, an ordinary fixed-mask derivation needs reconsideration. F1 legal masks are task rules computed from state, not trainable action preferences.

def score_vector(logits, action):
    z = np.asarray(logits,dtype=float)
    if z.ndim != 1 or not len(z) or not np.isfinite(z).all() or type(action) is not int or action not in range(len(z)):
        raise ValueError('finite logits and valid integer action required')
    p = np.exp(z-z.max()); p /= p.sum()
    score = -p.copy(); score[action] += 1
    return p,score

Recap and self-check

Is a softmax score an action-value gradient?

No. It differentiates the policy’s likelihood. Return or advantage will weight it when estimating the objective gradient.
