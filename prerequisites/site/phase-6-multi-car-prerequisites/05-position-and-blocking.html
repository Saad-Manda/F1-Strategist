Why "Position = Sorted by Time" Kills the Undercut

Not a bug in the usual sense: a missing rule that makes this phase's most interesting goal impossible, however long training runs.

Your call · the undercut, blocking on

Turn blocking on: a car within 1 s of the one ahead drives at its pace unless a pass succeeds. The rival still stops on lap 9. In the plan's race, stopping on lap 6 instead of lap 9 cost you 0.40 s.

What does stopping on lap 6 instead of lap 9 do now?

The undercut in real racing

A driver stops before a rival on similar pace. The fresh tyres give laps the rival, still on worn ones, can't match. By the time the rival stops too, the stop has turned into track position. It works because of one fact about real racing: the rival can't drive around the car in front of it. Track position lost in traffic is hard to get back, and that is why the undercut is worth timing.

What the plan's pseudocode computes

The plan's _update_positions() recomputes every position each lap by sorting the cars' total times. If nothing stops a faster car from going straight past a slower one, position is a readout of who has used less time, and there is nothing to gain from timing a stop against a rival's. "Stop early while you're faster" and "be faster" are the same statement, because being ahead or behind never costs or saves time by itself. The model has no notion of being stuck.

That also leaves attempt_overtake nothing to do. A pass is worth something only if it releases you from being stuck behind a car, and nothing in this model ever holds a car back.

The fix: one line that makes position a resource

A car close behind another that hasn't passed it is held, at best, to the pace of the car ahead plus page 04's wake:

if gap_to_car_ahead < OVERTAKE_ATTEMPT_GAP and not overtake_succeeded: lap_time = max(own_lap_time, car_ahead_lap_time + dirty_air_penalty(gap))

Positions are still read off total time. What changes is that the lap times going into the totals now depend on who is stuck behind whom, and a pass is how you get out. The Pit Wall's blocking switch is this line, with the wake in the lap time.

Stopping on lap 6 takes 228.14 s and finishes ahead in every race. Stopping on lap 9, with the rival, takes 231.97 s and finishes behind in every race. Stopping together keeps the order: you rejoin about 0.6 s behind, where its wake cancels your 0.3 s edge, and no pass is ever tried. The undercut is worth 3.83 s.

In the plan's race the same two plans differ by 0.40 s the other way, because lap 6 is a slightly worse lap to stop on and nothing else. One line made the rival's stop lap part of your best call.

Deep diveThe general shape of this gap70 words

This isn't an off-by-one or a typo. Every visible piece of a mechanism is there, pass probabilities, dirty air, positions, and the one rule that connects them to a consequence is missing. Reasonable parts can add up to nothing. The concrete check is a test that a faster car stuck behind a slower one gains no time until it passes, rather than assuming the rule is there because the parts around it are.

Root causeFound. Without a rule that holds a car behind the one ahead, track position costs nothing and the rival's stop lap appears in none of your lap times. Why does the stop's timing matter now?

3 · What it means

With the line in place a pit stop does two things: it costs 20 s, and it gets you out of traffic. That second job, which did not exist a moment ago, is what an undercut uses.

In a race without the blocking line, what can a successful overtake change?
Worked solution

No blocking: 30 × 0.5 − 20 = −5, so FAST finishes 5.0 s behind for any pit tick. Blocking, level start: FAST is held to SLOW's pace from tick 0 until its stop, so the gap is 5 + 0.5 × tick: 7.5 at tick 5, 17.5 at tick 25, smallest (5.0) at tick 0. A pass with no blocking has nothing to release a car from.
