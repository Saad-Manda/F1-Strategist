Backward Induction

The last lap is the easiest call in the race, because nothing comes after it. The oracle starts there.

Your call · lap 8

The oracle must choose between staying out and boxing for B on lap 8. Each choice changes every lap after it.

Which part of the race does it work out first?

Why start at the flag?

On the last lap a stop only adds time, so the oracle stays out unless the two-compound rule forces a stop. The value of each lap-20 situation is that lap's time. Now lap 19 is easy too. Each call costs its lap time plus the value of where it leaves you on lap 20, which you already have. Step back one lap at a time, and each lap's calls are priced by the lap after it:

V_21(s) = 0 nothing after the flag for lap t = 20 down to 1, for every situation s on lap t: V_t(s) = min over legal calls a of [ cost(s, a) + E[ V_t+1(s′) ] ] π_t(s) = the call that gives that minimum

This is Phase 1's Bellman equation with the circle broken. In general the V on the left depends on V on the right, which depends on more V, and you iterate until the numbers settle. A race has a last lap, so every right-hand side refers only to the next lap, already solved. One pass back from the flag gives the value of every situation.

Work it: a 4-lap race by hand

One tyre, two calls each lap, no luck. Lap time grows with tyre age, and a stop costs 6 s on the lap you make it and gives you a new tyre for the next:

lap_time(age) = 10 + 3·(age − 1) age 1 = a new tyre cost(age, STAY) = lap_time(age) next age = age + 1 cost(age, PIT) = lap_time(age) + 6 next age = 1 the race starts on lap 1 with age 1

Why the one-lap view gets it wrong

A policy that compares only this lap's cost stays out every lap of the 4-lap race, since STAY is always 6 s cheaper on the lap itself. That is the never-pit race, 58 against 52. On the Pit Wall's lap 8, staying out is 4 s cheaper on the lap and 0.68 s dearer over the race. The stop pays later, in laps a one-lap view cannot see.

Deep diveWhy it is called dynamic programming90 words

The recursion uses two properties. Optimal substructure: the best plan from lap t on is built from the best plan from lap t + 1 on, so a later decision is never reconsidered once its value is known. Overlapping subproblems: many different histories reach the same situation, and V_t(s) is computed once and reused by all of them. The table is the memo. Recompute it for every path instead and the same problem grows exponentially: the 4-lap race has 24 = 16 call sequences, a 20-lap race over a million.

Deep diveBackward induction vs. value iteration100 words

The plan's resource map points to Sutton & Barto chapter 4, which covers dynamic programming for tasks with no fixed end and a discount. There, value iteration sweeps every state again and again using the current estimate of V, and reaches the true values only in the limit, because there is no V_(T+1) = 0 to anchor a single pass. Backward induction is the finite-horizon special case. A race has a known length, so one pass gives the exact values with no iteration. The chapter's ideas carry over; the fixed horizon is what makes them exact here.

Backward induction, two ways

First fill a value table by hand, for the page's toy race with new numbers. Then implement backward induction for a different problem and prove that it finds the same answer as trying every possible plan.

Warm-up, on paper · the page's race with new numbers

The same 4-lap race as the worked example, with a gentler tyre and a cheaper stop: lap_time(age) = 10 + 2·(age − 1) and pit_loss = 5. Everything else is unchanged: STAY costs lap_time(age) and the tyre is one lap older; PIT costs lap_time(age) + 5 and the next lap is on a tyre of age 1. Fill the table from lap 4 backward. Lap 4 is done for you: nobody pits on the last lap, so V₄(age) is 10, 12, 14, 16 for ages 1 to 4.

On which lap does the optimal race pit?
Worked solution for the warm-up

Lap 3. Age 1: STAY 10 + 12 = 22, PIT 15 + 10 = 25. Age 2: STAY 12 + 14 = 26, PIT 17 + 10 = 27. Age 3: STAY 14 + 16 = 30, PIT 19 + 10 = 29.

Lap 2. Age 1: STAY 10 + 26 = 36, PIT 15 + 22 = 37. Age 2: STAY 12 + 29 = 41, PIT 17 + 22 = 39.

Lap 1. STAY 10 + 39 = 49, PIT 15 + 36 = 51. So V₁(1) = 49: stay, pit on lap 2, stay, stay. Never pitting costs 52.
