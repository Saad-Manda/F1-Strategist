A plain sum has an arbitrary offset

For Q=[−2,−5,−3], the plain decomposition Q=V+A permits V=0, A=[−2,−5,−3] or V=−10, A=[8,5,7]. Adding c to V and subtracting c from every A leaves Q unchanged. Neither head's level is uniquely determined by that sum.

If we require the effective centered advantage to have mean zero, then V must equal mean(Q)=−10/3. Effective advantages become [4/3,−5/3,1/3]. Adding this common level recovers the original Q. This decomposition of Q into its mean and deviations is unique.

The raw head still has a null direction

In the implemented formula Q=V+Araw−mean(Araw), adding any common c to Araw has no effect because its mean also increases by c. Centering makes the effective deviations identifiable; it does not uniquely fix the raw advantage head or all neural parameters. The old source's claim of complete uniqueness conflated these two objects.

For example, V=−10/3, raw A=[−2,−5,−3] and the same V with raw A=[8,5,7] both produce the original Q. The raw means differ, but their centered vectors agree. Simply substituting V=0 into the mean-subtracted formula would instead produce [4/3,−5/3,1/3]; when converting a plain decomposition, move its mean into V too.

Follow one selected action's gradient

For A actions, the derivative of Q_j with respect to raw advantage A_k is 1−1/A if k=j and −1/A otherwise. With two actions, it is +0.5 for the chosen head output and −0.5 for the other. Thus loss on one chosen Q can change every raw advantage output at that state, even though it does not supply labels for unexecuted actions.

Under centering over all representable actions, V is their arithmetic mean Q, including illegal outputs. It is not automatically the optimal legal V*=max legal Q or the policy value Vπ=ΣπQ taught in Foundations. Likewise these architectural deviations are not a claim about a later policy-gradient advantage estimator. Use the same letter only with its stated definition.

Recap and self-check

Does the implementation force raw A=[1,3] to become a stored zero-mean head output?

No. It computes deviations [−1,1] during the forward combination. The raw output remains [1,3] and could be shifted without changing Q.
