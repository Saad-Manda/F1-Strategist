Map each readiness item to what it can establish

Saving every 1000 episodes and retaining at most 50 checkpoints tests cadence/eviction, not seed-policy quality or historical coverage. Sampling recent entries near .7 tests one distribution, not Nash convergence. Beating a random legal agent tests a sanity floor; it does not identify undercut/overcut reaction or competence against strong held-out styles.

A timing-difference histogram is a descriptive output. Independent stint-length modes can create two peaks, while weather and scenario difficulty can induce shared patterns. Record both actual pairings and a justified scenario-stratified null. Better still, intervene on a rival’s timing with matched exogenous conditions and test whether the learner’s response changes. Passing a mechanical intervention does not prove the behavior was learned.

The self-play lab’s matrix, copied probability snapshots and API checks are runnable tiny contracts. Its optional compare scripts and recorded reports must be labeled by their actual budgets and versions. No browser display is live Python training, and the course rebuild does not initiate a new self-play run.

Preparation is an acknowledgment

Review lifecycle endings, shared dynamics, pool initialization/one-entry sampling, frozen opponent storage and full physical evaluation. Preserve named historical and external opponents outside the eviction loop. Record uncertainty across whole races and independent training seeds. The module checklist acknowledges these concepts and application contracts; production training and strategic claims still need their own evidence.

What does a visible two-peak delta histogram prove by itself?

Only that the recorded marginal statistic has two peaks under that binning and dataset. Reaction, causality and generalization require further evidence.
