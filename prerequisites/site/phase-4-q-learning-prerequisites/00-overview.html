Phase 4 · Case brief

The Learner That Can't See the Window

Phase 4 builds agents that learn the race from experience. First, watch two of them learn it.

Below are two Q-learners, the kind this phase builds. They race the Pit Wall's 20-lap race over and over and keep a table of what each call was worth. They see the same races and the same random numbers. The only difference is the table. The fine learner has one cell per lap of tyre age and per lap to go. The coarse learner has one cell per 5 laps of each, the shape of the real plan's first table. Press Train to 20,000.

After 20,000 races the fine learner boxes for B on lap 8, the oracle's lap, and its strategy is 0.81 s from the best possible on average. The coarse learner boxes on lap 1, and three more times after that: 12.70 s from the best. Its gap was 9.87 s after 5,000 races. Training made it worse.

Why can't more training fix the coarse learner?

It has the same algorithm, the same races, and 400,000 updates. Is it the algorithm, the settings, or not enough races? And what should the real plan change before anyone trains on it?

You will collect the evidence one page at a time:

  1. The update rule: how a table learns from one lap at a time.
  2. Q-learning: the target it learns toward, run by hand.
  3. Exploration: is the coarse learner short of exploring?
  4. SARSA: the learner's cautious twin, and why the plan trains both.
  5. Bins: why a table needs them, and what too few and too many cost.
  6. The binning bug: what the coarse table can't tell apart. The maths of the case closes here.
  7. The build: you write the two learners, and measure them against the oracle.
  8. Back to the plan: the real table, the tests, and the verdict. The case closes.

Why learn when the oracle already knows?

Phase 3.5's oracle solved the race because it was handed the model: every lap-time formula and every probability. Agents in this project are not. Q-learning and SARSA see one lap at a time, (s, a, r, s′), and must work out a good strategy from that alone, as a rookie strategist would. The oracle stays on as the ruler: every learner here is scored by its gap to the oracle's optimum, as the two above are.

What Phase 4 does and does not do

This set teaches tabular temporal-difference control, Q-learning and SARSA, and the binning problem that a table forces on this project. Neural networks, experience replay and target networks are not covered, even in preview. They belong to Phase 5.
