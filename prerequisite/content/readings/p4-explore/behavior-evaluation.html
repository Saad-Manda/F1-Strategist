Training performance answers a different question

A learner may deliberately make exploratory choices while improving its table. Training return measures that changing behavior, including exploration. Evaluation of a learned greedy policy freezes the table and sets epsilon to zero. These can produce different scores without a contradiction.

In a two-step shortcut world, START chooses RISK or SAFE for reward zero. SAFE then gives 3. RISK has GOOD giving 6 and SLIP giving −10. If GOOD is the unique greedy RISK action and epsilon is 0.2, the behavior probabilities are [0.9,0.1]. RISK's behavior value is 0.9×6+0.1×(−10)=4.4; its greedy value is 6. A run's sampled return is either 6 or −10, not necessarily 4.4.

Freeze all sources of learning behavior

Evaluation must not update Q, decay training counters, reuse a pending SARSA action, or allocate/change table entries accidentally through a defaultdict lookup. Define how unseen states are handled and whether tie-breaking is deterministic or seeded. Epsilon zero alone can still randomize equal maxima under uniform tie-breaking.

Use independent environment and policy random streams. Otherwise an extra exploratory draw can change tomorrow's weather, making a performance comparison confound policy and scenario changes. For reproducibility, record seeds, generator implementations, algorithm versions and action tie rules. A seed number by itself is not a complete experiment specification.

Evaluate on the task you intend to report

A policy trained on dry/no-SC curriculum episodes may be tested on the full task. That is a distribution shift worth labeling. Keep training, validation and final held-out scenario sets separate; repeated use of final test results to choose settings makes them part of model selection rather than an independent test.

Compare algorithms under a shared environment-seed set and report per-seed differences in physical time and constraint violations. More than one seed is useful because a favorable initialization or sampled route can dominate a single result. Equal training seeds do not force algorithms with different action-call patterns to see identical experience.

Recap and self-check

If greedy evaluation scores 6 and training averages about 4.4 at the RISK branch, which is wrong?

Neither need be wrong. They describe different policies. The report becomes misleading only if one is labeled as the other or if evaluation still changes the learner.
