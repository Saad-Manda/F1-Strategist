Phase 4 · Page 03 · Exploration

Exploration: ε-Greedy

A learner has to try calls it thinks are worse, or it never finds out it was wrong.

Your call · the plan's settings

The real plan lowers ε from 1.0 to 0.05 over 500,000 steps. A race at the reference circuit is 52 laps, one step per lap, and training runs 10,000 races.

In which race does ε reach its floor of 0.05?

Why greedy alone gets stuck

A learner that always takes the call its table rates best can lock in a mediocre one. If an early, unreliable estimate ranks a poor pit lap above the right one, it keeps choosing the poor lap, and the right lap's estimate never gets the visits that would correct it. This is the exploration/exploitation tradeoff Phase 1 named: use what looks best, or try something else to find out.

ε-greedy, defined

with probability ε: a call chosen at random, among the LEGAL calls with probability 1 − ε: the best call by the table, among the LEGAL calls

"Legal" matters in both branches. The random draw must come from the calls the mask allows, and the best call must ignore illegal calls' values (set to −∞ before the max) rather than pick one because its untrained value looks high.

Linear decay, with the plan's numbers

A fixed ε keeps exploring as hard at the end as at the start. The plan lowers it in a straight line:

ε_t = max( ε_end, ε_start − t · (ε_start − ε_end) / decay_steps ) step = (1.0 − 0.05) / 500,000 = 1.9 × 10⁻⁶ per lap

The numbers sayAbout race 9,615

500,000 steps at 52 per race is 500,000 / 52 ≈ 9,615 races, out of 10,000. The agent is still lowering ε for 96% of training. The plan's config comment calls it "~5K eps", which would be true of a 100-lap race. If the intent is to explore for the first half and refine in the second, decay_steps should be near 5,000 × 52 = 260,000.

Which is better is a question for measurement, not for the comment. The Learner below lets you measure it on the toy race.

Measure it

Open Settings and change how long exploration lasts, then train to 20,000. Over 20 learners with fine bins, a decay over the first 2,000 races ends with a median gap of 0.92 s (13 of 20 within 1 s), over 6,000 races 1.05 s (10 of 20), and over 15,000 races 1.24 s (6 of 20).

One more result. A fine learner that never explores at all, ε = 0 from the first race, ends with a median gap of 1.18 s, not stuck. Its table starts at 0 and every centred reward is negative, so any call it hasn't tried looks best (page 02's step 4), and it tries it. That is optimistic initialisation, the same lever as the plan's "+10" idea for pit calls.

The coarse learner doesn't respond the same way. Over 20 learners its median gap is 12.74 s with a decay over 2,000 races and 11.91 s over 15,000. With no exploring at all it does better, 4.92 s, and is still about four times the fine learner's gap. A shared cell holds a blend of every situation that lands in it, weighted by how often each one comes up, and exploring changes that mix. More or less exploring moves the coarse number about; neither fixes it.

Deep diveWhy masking belongs inside the random draw130 words, 1 table

"Random over all 6 actions" wastes exploration on calls that are illegal here, when perhaps only 3 are allowed and one of them is the stop the agent needs to discover. Over 10 exploratory visits to that state, the chance of trying the useful call at least once:

ExplorationOne drawAt least once in 10
Unmasked: random over all 61/6 ≈ 0.1671 − (5/6)¹⁰ ≈ 0.838
Masked: random over the 3 legal1/3 ≈ 0.3331 − (2/3)¹⁰ ≈ 0.983

The gap grows in states that are visited rarely, the sparse cells of pages 05 and 06, and where more calls are illegal: with 2 legal calls out of 6, the masked draw finds the useful one three times as often as the unmasked one.

Deep diveDecaying too fast80 words

Lowering ε too soon locks in whatever the table preferred early, before enough states were visited to trust it: the greedy failure, delayed. If a learning curve flattens early at a mediocre level, check epsilon_decay_steps against the training budget before anything more exotic. The plan's risk table names the same fix: "increase epsilon_decay_steps to 1M". On the toy race the faster schedule did better, so the toy says to measure, not which way to move.

Evidence so farExploration is not the coarse learner's problem: every schedule leaves it far off, and switching exploration off moves it to 4.92 s, not to the fine learner's 1 s. The plan's decay lasts to race 9,615, not 5,000. Next: SARSA, the learner that prices its own exploring.
