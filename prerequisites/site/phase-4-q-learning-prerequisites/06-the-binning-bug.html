The Binning Bug: When Coarseness Erases the Decision

A table that runs, trains and has a sensible size can still be unable to hold the right strategy. The real plan's first table is one.

Your call · the coarse learner

After 20,000 races the coarse learner boxes on lap 1. The oracle boxes on lap 8. In the coarse table, laps 6 to 9 on the starting tyres are one cell.

Train it another 60,000 races?

What the coarse table can't tell apart

The coarse learner's cells are 5 laps of laps-to-go by 5 laps of tyre age. On the starting tyres, age equals the lap, so the first stint crosses these cells: laps 1–4, lap 5, laps 6–9, lap 10, laps 11–14, lap 15, laps 16–19, lap 20. The oracle's stop on lap 8 is inside the laps 6–9 cell, and one cell holds one call for all four laps. Stopping on lap 8 cannot be written in this table. The nearest it can hold is a stop on lap 6, the first lap of the cell. A search of the coarse table's strategies finds one 1.10 s from the optimum, stopping on lap 6.

Below, page 00's pair, trained to 20,000 races. A thick line marks where a coarse cell changes.

Why it learns worse than its table allows

The coarse learner doesn't reach 1.10 s. It ends 12.70 s off, boxing on lap 1. Each cell's number is a blend of every situation that shares it, and each update bootstraps from a neighbouring cell's blend (page 01's warning, come due).

On lap 1, its table rates boxing for B 1.88 s better than staying out. Played out with its own later calls, the stop is 0.47 s worse. The error comes from the cell the stop lands in: "on B, laps 2 to 5, tyres 1 to 4 laps old". Its four laps have real values 7.55 s apart, the table stores one number for all of them, and that number is 2.48 s kinder than lap 2's. A stop on lap 1 borrows the optimism.

Training more makes the blend more accurate as a blend. It never splits it. Over 20 coarse learners the median gap is 9.14 s after 5,000 races and 12.52 s after 20,000; over 10, it is 13.07 s after 40,000 and 12.95 s after 80,000.

Two losses stack. The table cannot hold the lap-8 stop, which costs at least 1.10 s. And learning from blended neighbours costs far more: 8 to 12 s on top, at every training length tried.

This is not underfitting. More races, a different learning rate, a longer exploration schedule: none of them gives back what the key threw away before the agent saw it.

The real plan's numbers

The plan's tire_age bin is min(int(age / 50 × 10), 9), which is age // 5. The Phase 0 audit measured 5 to 10 laps within 1 s of the oracle's best stop. If the best stop is lap 27, laps 27 and 29 share bin 5, and Q(bin 5, PIT) has to stand for both. The plan's Step 1 warning says so, and adopts 1 to 2 laps per tire_age bin (25 to 50 bins), finer near the cliff.

Deep diveWhat the fix costs, and the plan's warning90 words

Phase 0.5 requires the window of laps within 1 s of the best stop to be at most 12 laps wide, so a 5-lap bin is often a large share of the whole window. The plan's warning on Step 1: "The agent is structurally prevented from representing the optimum, and no amount of training fixes that." Going from 10 to 40 tyre-age bins multiplies the table by 4, about 33 MB in full, and the defaultdict only stores cells that are visited. The coarseness never bought back memory worth having.

Coarse tyre age alone barely hurts here, because on the first stint the exact lap gives the age away. It is the two together that erase the window, and fixing age to 2 laps while laps-to-go stays at 5 still leaves 5.76 s. The real race restarts tyre age at every stop, so don't read "age alone is harmless" into this. Read: the key is the whole tuple, and the plan's laps_remaining bins are about 4.3 laps wide.

The test this calls for

discretize_obs must give the oracle's best pit lap and its neighbours distinct keys. In the toy race, laps 7, 8 and 9 on the starting tyres are one coarse key and three fine ones. Without it, Phase 4 measures the binning, not the algorithm, and its gap to Oracle B means little.

Deep diveHow the 1.10 s was found100 words

A coarse table's greedy strategy is one call per cell. Starting from the oracle's call in each cell's first situation, a search changed one cell's call at a time and kept any change that lowered the exact expected race time (Phase 3.5's expectedTime), until no single change helped. It ended at 1.10 s above the optimum, stopping on lap 6. A search like this can miss a better combination, so 1.10 s is the best found, not a proven floor. Started from "stay out everywhere", the same search stopped at 9.15 s: the coarse table's strategies are hard to search, too.

So what is the real cost of finer tyre-age bins?
Worked solution

1. 10 bins: 2 (ages 25–29 and 30–32). 25 bins: 5. 40 bins: 6. Every lap has its own bin for certain only from 50 bins up; 47 and 49 happen to work for this window and 48 does not.

2. 12 × 5 × 40 × 3 × 3 × 2 × 8 × 2 = 691,200 states; × 6 × 8 bytes = 33.2 MB. The cost that matters is experience, not memory.
