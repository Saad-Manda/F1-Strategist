Propagate a distribution without drawing a trace

The distribution calculation averages every possible transition. It is reproducible without a random generator. Reuse the matrix validator and compute the probability of destination j by summing current-origin probability times its transition probability:

def advance(distribution, matrix):
    validate_matrix(matrix)
    validate_distribution(distribution)
    if len(distribution) != len(matrix):
        raise ValueError("distribution dimension mismatch")
    return [sum(p*matrix[i][j] for i,p in enumerate(distribution))
            for j in range(len(matrix))]

WEATHER = [[0.8,0.2],[0.3,0.7]]
one_step = advance([1,0], WEATHER)
two_steps = advance(one_step, WEATHER)
assert abs(two_steps[0] - 0.7) < 1e-10

A single sampled trace cannot reproduce those fractional probabilities at each lap: it occupies one state. Across many separately seeded traces, occupancy frequencies can approximate the propagated distribution when the simulated law matches it.

A distribution that stays unchanged

A stationary distribution satisfies dP=d: one transition leaves its probabilities unchanged. Write d=[x,1−x] for this two-state matrix. Its dry coordinate gives x=0.8x+0.3(1−x), hence 0.5x=0.3, so d=[0.6,0.4]. Check both coordinates: dry is 0.48+0.12=0.6 and wet is 0.12+0.28=0.4. This chain can reach either state from the other and has positive self-transition probabilities, so its marginal distributions approach that unique stationary distribution from either start. Other chains can have several stationary distributions or oscillating marginals; do not assume this behavior from row normalization alone.

Label the quantity you compare

Suppose dry lap cost is 10 and wet lap cost is 14. Expected next-lap cost starting dry is 0.8×10+0.2×14=10.8. One actual next lap costs either 10 or 14. The expectation is not a claim that every lap takes 10.8 seconds.

The expected sum of lap costs is the sum of their expectations even when successive weather states are dependent. Its variance also depends on correlations: persistent wet periods can create clustered high-cost laps. Do not treat all laps from one chain as independent race samples when estimating uncertainty in race totals.

Compare controlled policies fairly

To compare an oracle policy and another policy, run both under the same scenario settings and a paired set of environment seeds. Record total physical cost for each race, then form per-seed differences. Independent policy streams avoid action-sampling calls changing weather draws. Equal seeds still require matching environment draw schedules; if actions alter random-call counts, use explicit exogenous schedules or document the weaker pairing.

A measured average can lie above or below a true model expectation. Increasing independent race count reduces sampling uncertainty under appropriate finite-variance assumptions; it does not repair a biased model, policy information leak or wrong cost sign.

Recap and self-check

Should every sampled run score below a perfect-information policy's expected reward?

No. A lucky realization can exceed an expectation. Information bounds compare optimal expected values under aligned task assumptions, not every realized score against one average.
