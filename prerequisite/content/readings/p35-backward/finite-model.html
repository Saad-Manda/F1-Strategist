Time changes the best choice

At A with one decision left, DIRECT gives 3 while VIA gives only its immediate 1: the task deadline ends before FINISH. With two decisions left, VIA gives 5. The same physical label A therefore needs different values at different horizons. Let h mean decisions remaining and write V_h(s). The deadline boundary is V_0(s) = 0.

That boundary describes an intrinsic deadline, not an external collection cap. Reaching T also removes continuation, even when h is positive. Keep the terminal reward on the transition that enters T; do not store it as T's continuation and count it twice.

Enumerate what is actually possible

For horizon two, a rectangular storage scheme has three physical labels A, B, T times three horizon slices h = 0, 1, 2: nine cells. Some are irrelevant from the chosen start. From (A, 2), DIRECT reaches T and VIA reaches (B, 1); no action returns to A. Enumerating all cells is convenient, but reachability can reduce work in a larger model.

The action set can depend on state and time. A has DIRECT and VIA, B has FINISH, and T has none. A nonterminal state with no legal actions is a specification error unless you deliberately define it as failure, forced waiting or another explicit boundary. Do not silently assign it zero value.

The finite Bellman relationship

For deterministic transitions, let f(s,a) be the next state and r(s,a) its reward. Then Q_h(s,a) = r(s,a) + γ V_(h−1)(f(s,a)), and V_h(s) = max_(legal a) Q_h(s,a). Gamma discounts one decision into the future. This is the Foundations equation with the horizon index made explicit.

Cost minimization uses the same structure with positive cost c and a minimum instead of a maximum. With reward r = −c and gamma one, optimal return is negative optimal total cost. Choose a sign convention for an entire calculation rather than switching between minimum cost and maximum negative reward halfway through it.

Count computation as well as storage

Each horizon slice depends only on the previous slice. A planner retaining the policy at all times can keep two value slices while storing all chosen actions. Approximate work is horizon × states × legal actions × possible successors. A small state count with a huge branching factor can still be expensive. Finite enumeration alone is not a performance benchmark.

Recap and self-check

Is (A, 1) equivalent to (A, 2) for this task?

No. DIRECT is best at h = 1; VIA is best at h = 2. Omitting h loses information required to choose correctly.
