Where does that call come from?

Two ways to find a strategy

Phase 1's agent learns. It acts, gets a reward, and slowly works out what pays. Nobody shows it the lap-time formula or the safety-car odds. Q-learning, DQN and PPO, the methods of Phases 4, 5 and 9, all work this way. They are model-free: they never see P(s′ | s, a) written down.

But this race is no mystery to the person who wrote it. The lap time is a formula in dynamics.py and the reward is -lap_time. With the model written down you can plan: compute the best strategy from the model, with no trial and error and no sampling error. Dynamic programming is the classic way to plan, and it is what Phase 3.5 builds.

How big a race can you plan?

Exact planning prices every situation the race can be in. The Pit Wall's race has 3,360: 20 laps, two compounds, any tyre age up to the lap, safety car or not, both compounds used or not, wet or dry. The oracle prices all of them in about 10 ms.

The real race has six features: lap (about 55), compound (5), tyre age (about 55), compounds used (8), safety car (2) and weather (3). That is 55 × 5 × 55 × 8 × 2 × 3 = 726,000 situations, a few megabytes of table. Exact solving is a size argument: it works when every situation fits in memory. Phase 1 kept the state small and made of counts for this reason. A state full of continuous, finely measured quantities would have made this phase impossible.

On lap 8 the oracle prices both calls all the way to the flag: boxing for B is worth 152.66 s on average, staying out 153.34 s. It boxes. Both numbers come from the lap-time formula and the 6% safety-car chance, worked through all 3,360 situations. No race was played to get them. Page 02 shows how.

Option (c) is the trap. Nobody wrote "lap 8" anywhere. Change the pit loss or the cliff and the oracle's stop moves on its own.

What does planning buy?

First, a ruler. "My agent beat my rule" compares against a strawman of your own making. "My agent is 0.4 s from the best possible strategy" is a claim about the problem itself.

Second, the strongest test of the simulator you will write. The oracle cannot make a mistake; it can only report the best play in the model it was given. So if it finds a strategy that saves 200 s, the plan says, "the simulator has an exploit — and you found it before an agent did". Page 05's capstone breaks a model on purpose and watches the oracle find the hole.

The oracle evaluates; it never teaches

Training an agent to copy the oracle's calls is a real technique, imitation learning, but it is a different project. Phases 4, 5 and 9 set out to show that an agent can find good strategy from experience without being shown the answer. The oracle only marks the work.

Deep diveWhy most of the 726,000 situations never happen90 words

Many combinations cannot occur. A tyre cannot be older than the race so far, and the compound on the car is always among the compounds used. A reachability pass, run forward from the start before solving, marks the situations that can occur, and the backward pass visits only those. The plan expects it to cut the work 5 to 10 times. It is the biggest speed-up, and it also stops the oracle reporting values for situations that cannot happen. The Pit Wall's 3,360 already leave out tyre ages above the lap.
