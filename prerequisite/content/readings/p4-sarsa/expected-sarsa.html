Average the next choice instead of sampling it

Expected SARSA uses y = r + γ Σ_(legal a′) π(a′|s′) Q(s′,a′). The sum averages behavior-policy action choices at the sampled successor state. It does not automatically average the environment's random transition: that successor and reward still came from one observed transition.

For RISK values [6,−10], epsilon 0.2 gives probabilities [0.9,0.1]. With reward zero and gamma one the expected target is 4.4, sampled SARSA can give 6 or −10, and Q-learning gives 6. Conditional on the fixed successor values and behavior policy, sampled SARSA's action-average equals Expected SARSA's target.

def expected_sarsa_target(reward, values, legal, gamma, epsilon, terminated):
    validate_distribution([gamma,1-gamma])
    if terminated:
        return reward
    row = probabilities(values,legal,epsilon)
    return reward + gamma*sum(p*q for p,q in zip(row,values))

assert abs(expected_sarsa_target(0,[6,-10],[True,True],1,0.2,False)-4.4) < 1e-10

This teaching helper assumes a finite reward; production validation should enforce it. Terminal transitions do not need a successor action distribution. The function's policy probabilities must use the same masks, epsilon and tie convention as the behavior you intend to evaluate.

The risky-push threshold is a calculation

For s2 PIT=−8 and PUSH_MORE=−30, the behavior continuation is (1−ε/2)(−8)+(ε/2)(−30)=−8−11ε. PUSH at s1 therefore has value −9−11ε. PIT_NOW is −12. PUSH remains preferable when ε<3/11≈0.273; at epsilon 0.05 it is −9.55, still better than −12.

Thus lower SARSA values do not guarantee a different greedy route or a cautious route at every epsilon. At fixed epsilon and appropriate learning assumptions, the behavior continuation matters; during decay the target policy itself changes. Constant-step finite runs can fluctuate around estimates.

Variance and policy assumptions

Averaging next actions removes that action-sampling noise conditional on the same state and values. Reward/transition randomness remains. This does not prove universal sample-efficiency superiority, and computing the whole action distribution has a cost. If the target distribution is another policy, label the method's policy relationship explicitly rather than assuming every expectation is on-policy.

Recap and self-check

At epsilon zero, with legal values [6,−10], do Expected SARSA and Q-learning have the same one-step target?

Yes. All probability goes to the legal maximum. Their equality here follows the policy choice, not the word “expected.”
