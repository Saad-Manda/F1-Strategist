From One Car to N

Nothing about how the agent's own tyres or fuel behave changes. What's new is that the agent's outcome now depends on cars it doesn't control.

What breaks, and what doesn't

Phase 1's state has 16 features, every one of them about the agent's own car — its tyre compound, tyre age, laps remaining, the weather, whether a safety car is out. Adding four more cars to the same race changes none of that. compute_lap_time is unchanged: the agent's own tyres degrade exactly the way Phase 0's calibration says they do, regardless of who else is on track. What's new is a second question layered on top of the first: it's no longer enough to drive your own race well, because now there's a notion of relative outcome — position, gap to the cars around you — that depends on choices the agent never makes.

Three new state features

The real plan adds exactly three: gap_to_car_ahead, gap_to_car_behind (both in seconds, normalized by a 60-second cap), and position_normalized (position / n_cars). The observation grows from Phase 1's 16 dimensions to N_OBS_MULTI = N_OBS_SINGLE + N_NEW_FEATURES = 19.

Why these three, and not — say — the full ordered list of every rival's tyre age and compound: the same Markov-sufficiency argument Phase 1 page 06 made about the single-car state applies again. The agent's next decision only needs to know what's immediately relevant to it: is there a car close enough ahead to be worth attacking or close enough behind to be a threat, and roughly how well is the race going overall. The rest of the field's internal state (what tyres rival #3 is on, four cars back) doesn't change what the agent should do this lap, so it doesn't belong in the state any more than cumulative_race_time_so_far belonged in Phase 1's.

Consequence for the Phase 5 network

The observation width changing from 16 to 19 isn't just a documentation detail — it means DuelingDQN's first Linear layer, sized for a 16-wide input, has to be rebuilt for 19. A checkpoint trained on the single-car environment cannot be reloaded as-is for Phase 6; a fresh network has to be constructed with the new input width and trained from scratch. This is worth knowing before training day, not discovering as a shape-mismatch error.

Rivals as fixed scripts, not learners

The other four (or however many) cars in the race are not RL agents. They follow deterministic, pre-defined pit-stop plans — a strategy is just a starting compound and a list of (lap, new_compound) pairs, chosen before the race and never adapted mid-race based on what the agent does. This is a deliberate simplification with a name and a payoff: it sidesteps the multi-agent non-stationarity problem entirely (page 07 explains exactly what that problem is and why avoiding it here is the right call), which is precisely why fixed rivals are the sensible first step before Phase 8's self-play, where rivals do learn and adapt.

From the agent's point of view, a fixed-script rival's behavior is just one more piece of the environment it needs to react to — no different in kind from the weather trace or the safety-car process it already has to handle. All of it is given, not something the agent's actions can change the rules of.
