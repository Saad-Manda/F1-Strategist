What does that say?

What is the real oracle's state?

A small tuple that can be hashed and enumerated, not a vector shaped for a network:

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class DPState:
    lap: int              # 1..total_laps
    compound: int         # 0..4  (SOFT/MEDIUM/HARD/INTER/WET)
    tyre_age: int         # 1..total_laps
    used_mask: int        # 0..7 -- bits for SOFT/MEDIUM/HARD
    sc_active: int        # 0 or 1
    weather: int          # 0..2  -- Oracle B only; fixed per lap in Oracle A

Each field is something your toy solver already has: lap is the backward-pass index, tyre_age stage 1's age, sc_active stage 2's, weather stage 4's. compound and used_mask are the real rules' version of stage 3's has_pitted. The plan sizes the table at "≈ 363,000 states", but its own six factors multiply to 55 × 5 × 55 × 8 × 2 × 3 = 726,000, twice that. Either fits in a few megabytes, and the reachability pass cuts it 5 to 10 times before solving.

The oracle works in cost, total race time to make small; the agent maximises reward = -lap_time. The same calls win either way. The plan negates once, where the oracle's policy is wrapped for the evaluation harness, and nowhere inside the solver.

How is the oracle scored?

DPOraclePolicy wraps the solved table behind act(obs, action_mask), the interface every agent has, so evaluate.py's paired-seed harness runs it unchanged. Its race time comes from rolling it out in the real environment with noise on, not from reading V[s₀]. V[s₀] is the expectation under the model. The rollout mean is a measurement, with luck in it, and the two should agree within that luck. Every results table from Phase 4 on gains one column:

optimality_gap(agent) = mean_race_time(agent) − mean_race_time(oracle_B)

in seconds, with a paired bootstrap confidence interval over the shared seeds. "Paired" is what tames the luck of page 03: every policy meets the same safety cars. In the toy race, a "box for B on lap 8" baseline is only 0.23 s slower than the oracle on average, yet over 200 sets of 500 paired seeds its mean never once beats the oracle's.

Deep diveWhy one shared implementation60 words

An oracle that prices a different race from the one agents train in does not fail loudly. It writes confident, wrong numbers into a results table. So the plan routes every lap cost through dynamics.compute_lap_time (with noise off, which page 03 showed is exact) and every legality check through dynamics.compute_action_mask, the function F1StrategyEnv.action_masks() also calls. Neither is restated in the oracle.

  • src/env/weather_markov.py. Page 04's count-and-divide fit per circuit, with the uniform-row fallback for a regime never seen; the fit-quality report; and sampling, shared by Oracle B and the environment's "markov" mode.

Re-run it whenever dynamics.py or the calibration changes: a policy cached against older physics is the same silent drift as a second implementation.

What the tests catch

Each test in tests/test_dp_oracle.py guards one way this phase can go wrong:

  • test_oracle_no_absurd_exploit: within 15% of the best hand-computed one-stop plan. Your stage 5. The plan calls it "the highest-value test in the repository": it catches a wrong-sign warm-up penalty before anyone blames DQN.
  • test_oracle_value_matches_rollout: V[s₀] and the mean of 500 rollouts agree within 3 standard errors. It catches wrong expectation weights, or a model that has drifted from the environment.
  • test_dp_action_mask_matches_env: on 1,000 sampled reachable states, the oracle's mask equals env.action_masks().
  • test_oracle_a_dominates_oracle_b: knowing the weather never makes an exact solver slower (page 04).
  • test_oracle_respects_two_compound_rule and test_oracle_pit_count_plausible: the rule binds, and the oracle makes 1 to 3 stops on a dry circuit.
  • test_oracle_beats_every_baseline: the oracle's mean is at or below every Phase 3 baseline's on the shared seeds.

Case 2 · the verdict

The oracle is best on average, and three of the plan's lines must say so

How did you beat the optimal strategy? On luck. Your time is the oracle's expected time plus the cost of your calls plus luck (page 03). On race #257 your calls cost 2.47 s and your luck beat the oracle's by 4.83 s. "Optimal" means best on average over the dice, which is the most a strategist who cannot see the future can aim for.

And when an agent beats it? On one race, nothing follows. On the mean over paired seeds, with a confidence interval below zero, something is wrong, and the plan leaves three places for it to hide. The prerequisites do not edit the plan; these go to whoever implements Phase 3.5.

1. Which race is the gap measured in? Templates stay the environment's default, and Step 6 defines optimality_gap against Oracle B without naming a weather mode. Oracle B is the optimum of the Markov-weather race only. Under replayed templates, an agent that recognises the template knows the weather to come, which is foresight B does not have: worth 1.37 s on average in the toy race. Measure the gap with weather_mode="markov".

2. Compare the oracles on the same weather. test_oracle_a_dominates_oracle_b holds when Oracle A's value on a template is compared with Oracle B's policy scored on that same template. Compared with B's V[s₀], it fails for two correct oracles whenever the template is wetter than average: on 871 of 2,000 sampled histories in the toy race. The Definition of Done's "value of perfect foresight (Oracle A − Oracle B)" has the same need: a paired difference per template, and in race times B − A, which is positive.

3. Which standard error? test_oracle_value_matches_rollout allows 3 × "noise standard error". It has to be the standard error of the 500 race times themselves, safety cars and weather included. In the toy race there is no lap noise at all, and the oracle's race times still spread with a standard deviation of 8.19 s: 3 standard errors over 500 races is 1.10 s, and the test passed 40 of 40 such blocks. A tolerance built from lap noise alone would be zero there, and a correct oracle would fail every run.

Worked solution

1. 70 × 5 × 70 × 8 × 2 × 3 = 1,176,000. Divided by 726,000 that is 1.62, which is (70 / 55)².

2. Unreachable: tyre older than the race so far; fitted compound missing from the used set; a never-changed tyre with two compounds used. Reachable: the lap-40 state and the start state.

3. B, A, B, A. The oracle is scored by rolling its policy out in the real environment.

4. Wrong-sign warm-up: test_oracle_no_absurd_exploit. Disagreeing mask: test_dp_action_mask_matches_env. Wrong expectation weights: test_oracle_value_matches_rollout. Foresight slower than no foresight: test_oracle_a_dominates_oracle_b. Missing terminal penalty: test_oracle_respects_two_compound_rule.

5. Exact, because the noise is additive and zero-mean. Re-run when the physics or calibration changes. The reachability pass is for speed and for not reporting impossible states; the value at the start state does not depend on it.

6. Compare the oracles on the same weather; measure the gap in Markov-weather mode; take the standard error from the rollouts themselves.
