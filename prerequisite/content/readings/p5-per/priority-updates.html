Trace one replay cycle

  1. Insert a copied transition and an explicit raw priority.
  2. Compute powered masses, normalize the retained distribution, and sample indices under the documented replacement or stratified law.
  3. Gather each sampled transition and its probability-derived weight; retain duplicate indices if the law allows them.
  4. Compute detached bootstrap targets and current selected predictions.
  5. Form per-item loss, multiply by detached weights, average and update only online parameters.
  6. Use detached pre-update TD errors to replace sampled raw priorities with |error|+epsilon.
  7. Synchronize the target according to its own update schedule.

dqn_step returns detached pre-update td_error. update_priority turns each scalar into a raw priority; a mass-storing tree then applies alpha once at its leaf. Do not feed weighted loss or squared TD error into a contract defined for absolute unweighted TD error. Loss and priorities may use different transforms deliberately.

Duplicates and a sum tree need explicit semantics

If an index appears twice, define how its proposed updates combine. For identical transitions in a deterministic plain model their pre-update errors match. With stochastic layers or differing targets they may not; choosing maximum absolute error, a last-write convention or another rule changes the algorithm. A tree parent holds the sum of descendant masses; updating a leaf propagates its mass difference to ancestors. This permits logarithmic index retrieval in a properly implemented tree, but does not remove the need to audit raw-versus-powered units.

The project plan divides total mass into batch-sized segments and draws within each segment. That stratified scheme can still repeat an entry. It is not the pack's independent with-replacement law, though its aggregate expected allocation follows proportional masses. Retrieve at valid boundaries, avoid unoccupied leaves, and keep the probability used in weights consistent with the sampler.

Recap and self-check

Do high priorities identify only rare pit transitions?

No. Any high-error item receives priority, including routine or noisy events. Inspect allocation and compare uniform replay with matched budgets before attributing an improvement to pit coverage.
