Phase 6 Multi-Car Prerequisites

Everything — and only what — you need to build the multi-car environment and rule-based rivals described in phase-6-multi-car-rivals.md, assuming you already have the Phase 1, Phase 3.5, Phase 4, and Phase 5 prerequisite sets and nothing more.

Why this phase is different from everything before it

Every phase through Phase 5 had exactly one car on track. The state, the reward, and the very notion of "doing well" were all about that one car's own race — a faster lap was always simply better, no matter what anyone else on track was doing. Phase 6 is the first time another agent's behavior enters the picture at all, even though that other agent is a fixed script, not something learning alongside the RL agent. That single change — one more car existing, with its own pace and its own pit stops, sharing the same track — turns out to touch the state, the reward, and the dynamics all at once, and each of those three gets its own trouble spot in this set.

Scope, precisely

This set teaches exactly what phase-6-multi-car-rivals.md needs: the multi-car state extension (gaps to the cars ahead and behind, race position), the reward-shaping problem that a "finish as high as possible" goal creates instead of "go as fast as possible," potential-based shaping as the principled version of that shaping, a probabilistic overtaking and dirty-air model, and — because the phase's own audit found something serious enough to teach directly rather than leave buried in a plan — the finding that the plan's pseudocode, as written, has no way for the undercut to ever emerge, plus the six smaller bugs that ship alongside that design gap. It does not cover self-play, PettingZoo, or anything else from Phase 8/9, beyond one deliberate exception: a short dedicated page on non-stationarity, scoped tightly to why fixed-script rivals sidestep it, since the real plan names that as the reason this phase is the sensible step before Phase 8.

Read this before anything else

This set is unusual among the five built so far. The code it eventually points to — f1_multi_env.py, rival.py, overtaking.py — does not exist in src/ yet, because Phase 6 hasn't been implemented. Pages 9 and 10 read the plan's own pseudocode, not a real, running file, and part of what they teach is the project's own audit findings against that pseudocode: a design gap serious enough that, unfixed, this phase's most interesting goal (undercut/overcut strategy) literally cannot happen, plus six smaller bugs. Reading a plan critically, before a single line of it is implemented, is itself the skill this set is trying to build alongside the RL content.

How to use this alongside the Phase 6 plan

Read pages 1–7 once, in order — they build one continuous argument, from what the state and reward gain when a second car exists, through the principled and unprincipled ways to shape a reward around that, the model for what happens when cars get close to each other, the single design gap that determines whether any of it can produce real racecraft, and the six bugs riding alongside that gap. Page 8 is hands-on: build a tiny version of the exact failure and fix yourself, on a toy race small enough to reason about by hand. Page 9 then re-derives the same concepts using nothing but this project's actual state shape, reward configs, and rival strategies. Page 10 is the closest thing to a line-by-line reading guide for the plan's pseudocode itself — and, unusually for this project's documentation, a guide to reading a plan skeptically rather than trusting it. Keep page 11 open as a reference while you work.
