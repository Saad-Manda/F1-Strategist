What happens to your lap-7 plan?

The assumption everything so far relies on

The convergence arguments behind tabular Q-learning (Phase 4) and DQN (Phase 5) assume a stationary environment: the same state and action give transitions from the same distribution, whenever they are visited. Every guarantee this course has used takes that for granted.

How learning opponents break it

Through Phase 5 the only thing that changes its behaviour over time is the agent itself. Put a second learner in the same race and, from the first one's point of view, the environment now includes the other's policy. If that policy is changing as it learns, the dynamics the agent is trying to learn shift under it. A stop under the safety car that worked against last week's rival may fail against this week's, with nothing changed about the track, the tyres or the weather.

Against a lap-7 rival your lap-7 plan stops on the same lap, comes out behind and stays there: 231.27 s, behind in every race. The best answer to a lap-7 rival is now lap 8, 227.54 s. Your tyres didn't change; the rival did, and with blocking on its choice is part of your lap times.

In the plan's race your lap-7 plan takes 224.97 s against any rival. A learning rival there can't move your best plan at all, so the plan's race is stationary for the wrong reason: not because the rivals are fixed, but because they can't affect you.

Why fixed-script rivals sidestep this

RivalState follows a pit_laps and pit_compounds plan chosen before the race, with no learning anywhere in it. To the agent the rivals behave like the weather trace or the safety car: given, external, and not changing in response to what the agent does. That is what stationary means here, and Phase 4 and 5's theory applies unmodified. What grew going into Phase 6 is the state (page 01) and the reward (pages 02 and 03), not the kind of environment.

The sensible order, not a cop-out

Building gaps, blocking, passing and position rewards against rivals that hold still means each piece can be debugged on its own, without asking whether an oddity is a bug or a rival that changed strategy mid-training. Phase 8 adds learning opponents once the interaction model is known to work. That only pays off if the interaction model is there to test: page 05's line is what makes a rival's choice reach your lap times at all.

Evidence so farWith blocking on, a rival moving its stop from lap 9 to lap 7 costs a lap-7 plan 3.17 s and the finish. With it off, nothing a rival does reaches you, so Phase 8's learning rivals would have nothing to teach. Next: you build the two-car race yourself.

A target that moves

The smallest game that shows page 07's point. You learn when to pit against an opponent, once against a fixed script and once against an opponent that copies what you have been doing. Against the script your values settle and the textbook estimate is nearly exact. Against the adapting one your choice flips dozens of times and nothing settles, though the rules never change.

Environment: a repeated two-choice game (pit EARLY or LATE; +1 for choosing differently from the opponent, −1 for the same), 4,000 rounds, ε-greedy.
