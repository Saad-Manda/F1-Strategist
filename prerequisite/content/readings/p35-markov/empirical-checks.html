Check rows, intervals and sampling separately

A good test starts with deterministic witnesses. Reject a nonsquare matrix, negative or nonfinite probabilities and rows with missing mass. For dry's [0.8,0.2] row, supplied draws 0, 0.7999, 0.8 and 0.9999 should select 0, 0, 1 and 1. Zero-probability columns remain unselected. Those tests isolate interval arithmetic without relying on a random seed happening to hit a boundary.

Then check seeded reproducibility and many-draw behavior. If 1000 independent draws are made from the fixed dry row, expected wet count is 200, with binomial standard deviation sqrt(1000×0.2×0.8)≈12.65. A count 207 is ordinary variation; demanding exactly 200 is an incorrect test.

Count actual origin opportunities

For full chain traces, count transitions by origin. If dry occurred 800 times as a predecessor and wet 200, their row estimates have different precision. Normalize by each origin count, not total trace length. Initial-state frequencies and terminal laps are not outgoing-transition counts. Never connect separate episodes while fitting.

For a well-sampled row, an approximate proportion standard error is sqrt(p(1−p)/n). Here n is that row's sample count. A normal approximation is unreliable for tiny counts or rare outcomes; an empirical zero with two observations does not imply zero uncertainty. Report counts alongside estimates and use justified intervals or simulation-based checks for the data-generating process.

Check predictions on held-out data

Split historical races before fitting, ideally respecting the circuit and temporal grouping needed by the experiment. On held-out transitions, compare observed frequencies and predicted probabilities. Log likelihood adds log(P[origin][destination]); a zero assigned to an observed event yields negative infinity. That exposes an unsupported zero rather than being a reason to secretly clip it after inspecting the test set.

Smoothing or a different weather model can be legitimate choices selected on training/validation evidence. Freeze them before final evaluation. Fit quality and simulator consistency are distinct: a sampler can faithfully reproduce a poorly estimated matrix.

Can matching a sampled count prove that the weather model matches real races?

No. It tests sampling under the assumed matrix. Held-out fit and state-sufficiency checks address whether that matrix is an appropriate model of the data.
