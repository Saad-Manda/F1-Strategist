Why a table needs bins

On pages 01 to 04, s was a label: s1, s2. In code, self.Q is a dictionary keyed by s, so s must be hashable and come from a set you can count. The real environment hands out a 16-dimensional float32 vector: laps remaining, tyre age, track temperature and more, each a decimal. Two observations a hair apart would be two keys that never share a value, and a table learns nothing it can reuse.

Binning (or discretization) turns each continuous feature into a few buckets, "tyre age 20 to 24 laps" rather than "22.7 laps", so that nearby situations share one cell.

Too few, too many

Binning throws information away, and how much is a design choice that can fail both ways:

  • Too few bins. Different situations share one cell, and the agent cannot make different calls in them, however long it trains. Page 06 shows this on the real plan's first table.
  • Too many bins. The same experience is spread over more cells, each visited less, and page 01 showed that one visit moves an estimate only a fraction of the way. Ten times the cells is roughly a tenth of the visits per cell.

No bin count makes the tension go away; you choose where to sit on it, coarse where a decision doesn't depend on the difference, fine where it does. Phase 5 ends the tension a different way: a neural network generalises between similar situations without bucketing them first.

The numbers sayThe same

Tyre age only ever takes whole values, so each age lands in one of its ten cells and the other nine are never visited. The visited cells, and the visits to each, are the ones the fine learner already had. A defaultdict never even creates the empty ones.

The cost of bins is counted in situations that occur, not in cells declared. After 3,000 races one fine learner on the Learner has touched 657 cells, its coarse twin 53.

How many cells is the plan's table?

The real plan bins the observation into eight dimensions:

laps_remaining → 12 bins tire_compound → 5 bins (SOFT, MEDIUM, HARD, INTER, WET) tire_age → 10 bins weather_regime → 3 bins (DRY, DAMP, WET) track_temp → 3 bins safety_car → 2 bins compounds_used → 8 bins (which of the 3 dry compounds have run) mandatory_pit → 2 bins total = 12 × 5 × 10 × 3 × 3 × 2 × 8 × 2 = 172,800

This is a budget, not an enumeration. Phase 3.5's 726,000 situations were every state the oracle had to solve. 172,800 is how many cells this scheme could ever produce, and most will be visited rarely or never. As a full table it is 172,800 × 6 calls × 8 bytes, about 8.3 MB, so memory was never what forced the bins to be coarse.
