A possible interruption is not a known interruption

At lap three, age two, suppose a safety car starts next lap with probability 0.25. The next-lap cost is 20 if interrupted and 16 if green. Its expectation is 0.25×20 + 0.75×16 = 17. Today's green lap costs 13, so the STAY branch costs 30 in expectation, compared with 29 in the deterministic case.

This calculation averages consequences before the event is known. It does not choose the cheaper outcome, and a sampled trajectory need not cost exactly 30. Reuse Foundations' rule: probabilities must be finite, nonnegative, exhaustive and sum to one; missing probability mass is a model error rather than permission to silently normalize it away.

A hazard is conditional on the current situation

A per-green-lap hazard q means P(SC_next=1 | SC_now=0)=q. It is not the probability of at least one interruption during an entire race. For k consecutive opportunities with independent start trials and no intervening active periods, the chance of no start is (1−q)^k.

Once active, this model uses a separate continuation probability. With geometrically distributed duration of mean d laps, each active lap has probability 1/d of ending, so continuation is 1−1/d. At d=3, continuation is 2/3. This is a memoryless modeling assumption: if duration has another law, active/not-active alone may omit relevant time-since-start information.

Combining two processes needs an assumption

Suppose, conditional on today's state, next SC probability is 0.25 and next wet-weather probability is 0.2. If conditionally independent, the four outcomes have weights green/dry 0.6, green/wet 0.15, SC/dry 0.2 and SC/wet 0.05. Their sum is one. A correlation between rain and interruptions would invalidate the product; then specify a joint conditional table instead.

Conditional independence means these next draws are independent given the modeled current information. It does not assert that whole race histories, lap costs or action choices are independent. A lap cost can depend on both random outcomes even when their generating draws are independent.

Recap and self-check

Does a hazard of 0.06 guarantee exactly three safety cars in a 50-lap race?

No. It describes each eligible green-lap opportunity. Active periods remove opportunities, and sampling produces variable counts. Even 50 independent opportunities would only have expected start count three, not a guaranteed count.
