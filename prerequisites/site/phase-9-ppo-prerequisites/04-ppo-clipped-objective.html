Phase 9 · Page 04 · The clip

PPO's Clipped Objective

The trick that makes it safe to reuse one batch of races for several passes of updates.

Your call · one cell of the clip

PPO's objective for one sample is min( r · A, clip(r, 0.8, 1.2) · A ), where r is how much more likely the call has become since the batch was collected. Here r = 1.3 and A = −1.

What is the objective's value?

A small step in θ can be a large step in behaviour

Nothing in pages 01 to 03 stops one update from moving the policy too far. A policy that almost always stops on lap 8 can be pushed to almost never, and the next batch of races is then collected by the broken policy. PPO measures how far an update has moved each sample with the probability ratio against the policy that collected the batch:

rt(θ) = πθ(at | st) / πθ_old(at | st) LCLIP = min( rt At, clip(rt, 1 − ε, 1 + ε) At )

The ratio is only meaningful for data from a policy close to the current one, which is why PPO is on-policy: unlike Phase 5's replay buffer, old races go stale. The plan's ε is 0.2 (clip_range), so the band is [0.8, 1.2].

The clip binds only once r has moved past the band in the direction the advantage favours. From there, moving it further earns nothing, so the gradient on that sample is zero. Moving it back is never penalised.

The numbers say−1.3

With A < 0 the clip binds only below 0.8. At r = 1.3 the call has become more likely though it was worse than average; the unclipped −1.3 is the smaller term, and the gradient keeps pushing r back down.

That asymmetry is the whole design: the objective stops rewarding a push once it has gone far enough, and never stops a correction.

Ten passes per batch, and the brake in the case

The plan's n_epochs: 10 reuses each batch for ten passes, which plain policy gradient can't do safely. On the toy race, at ten passes the mini PPO ended 1.43 to 1.71 s from the optimum with the clip and 7.19 to 10.65 s without (centred, three seeds). Below, seed 0 of each.

So is the clip what holds the raw MaskablePPO still? No. At the end of the raw run its logs show a clip fraction of 0.0, no sample past the band at all, and an approximate KL between old and new policy of 0.0002 (centred: 0.007 and 0.001). The clip is the brake for updates that are too large. The raw run's updates are too small.

Deep diveWhat the clip does not do180 words

The clip zeroes the gradient once a ratio is past the band; it doesn't stop a step that starts inside the band from landing outside it. One step from r = 1 with pold = 0.1 and A = 5 lands at r ≈ 2.15 (lab p9-04 works it by hand), and on the toy race twenty clipped steps left one ratio at 4.95 (p9-cap, stage 5). What the clip guarantees is that later steps stop rewarding the push. SB3 logs train/clip_fraction (the share of samples whose ratio left the band) and train/approx_kl (how far the policy moved); its optional target_kl stops an update's remaining passes once the policy has moved too far, and is off by default.

One caution on the Training room's numbers: with the raw reward the mini PPO without the clip did better at ten passes on two seeds of three (1.93, 1.93, 6.46 s). The clip's value is clear on the centred runs, where the plan would use it; three seeds of a table learner are not a law.

Evidence so farThe clip limits updates that are too large. In the raw MaskablePPO run it never binds (clip fraction 0.0, KL 0.0002): its updates are too small, not too big. Next: what kind of failure this is.

Worked solution

The table for ε = 0.1: A = +2 gives 1.7, 2, 2.2, 2.2; A = −2 gives −1.8, −2, −2.3, −3.2. The gradient is zero in (c), (d) and (e). In (a) and (h) r is outside the band, but the unclipped term is smaller and the push is back towards the band.

Overshoot: each logit moves by 0.45, the difference by 0.9, p goes from 0.1 to 0.2146, and the ratio from 1 to 2.15 in one step. The clip does not undo it; it only stops further pushing. clip_fraction and approx_kl show it happening.
