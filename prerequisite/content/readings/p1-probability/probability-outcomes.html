Why this matters

The switch and chain had deterministic rules. A delivery route might instead succeed or be delayed. To compare decisions before those events happen, list the possible outcomes and the probability attached to each one.

A probability is a number from 0 to 1. Zero means impossible under the model; one means certain. A probability of 0.8 means an 80% chance, not a guarantee that every five consecutive trials contain exactly four successes.

Build a valid outcome row

Suppose a route succeeds with probability 0.8 and is delayed with probability 0.2. The outcomes are mutually exclusive: one cannot be both the success and delay category in this table. They are exhaustive: every modeled run belongs to one category. Their probabilities sum to 1.

def validate_distribution(probabilities):
    import math
    if not probabilities or any(
        not math.isfinite(p) or not 0 <= p <= 1
        for p in probabilities
    ):
        raise ValueError("probabilities must be finite and in [0,1]")
    if abs(sum(probabilities) - 1) > 1e-10:
        raise ValueError("probabilities must sum to 1")

A small tolerance accommodates floating-point rounding. It does not approve a row summing to 0.8 or silently divide it by its total. Before normalizing arbitrary counts, identify them as counts; after normalizing they become probabilities. A table of probabilities already claims to describe the complete distribution.

If “success” includes some delayed deliveries, those categories overlap. Adding their probabilities would double-count those runs. Choose a mutually exclusive partition, such as prompt delivery, delayed delivery, and failure.

Conditional probability and independence

A conditional probability describes an event given specified information. P(delay | rain) means the probability of delay when rain is known. The vertical bar means “given”; it is not division.

Suppose rain occurs with probability 0.25. Delay probability is 0.6 given rain and 0.1 given dry weather. The joint probability of rain and delay is 0.25 × 0.6 = 0.15. Dry weather and delay has probability 0.75 × 0.1 = 0.075. Adding these disjoint cases gives overall delay probability 0.225.

The general multiplication rule uses a conditional probability: P(X and Y) = P(X) P(Y | X). Only when X and Y are independent can you replace the second factor by P(Y). Independence means knowing one does not change the other's probability.

For an explicitly independent toy pair, rain probability 0.25 and a battery failure probability 0.1 give joint probability 0.025. That arithmetic assumes independence; real weather-related battery failures could violate it. Choosing independent random draws in code is itself a modeling decision.

Recap and self-check

Common mistake: multiplying unconditional probabilities despite evidence that the events depend on each other, or omitting one possible outcome before calculating an average.

The listed probabilities are 0.7 and 0.1. Should a validator silently turn them into 0.875 and 0.125?

No. First investigate the missing 0.2 or the table's meaning. Normalizing silently changes the model and can hide a missing failure outcome.
