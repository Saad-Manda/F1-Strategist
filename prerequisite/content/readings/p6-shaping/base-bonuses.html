An attractive bonus can change the winner

Use the unchanged route lab: DIRECT has rewards [3], VIA+FINISH [0,4], VIA+WAIT [0,0]. At gamma one the base returns are 3,4,0, so VIA+FINISH wins. The ad-hoc bonus witness substitutes [3],[0,4],[0,5], making VIA+WAIT score 5 and win. Calling that third signal “progress” does not make it optimize finishing.

In the legacy negative-cost chain, A→B→C costs 3+1 while A→C costs 10. A flat pit bonus larger than the relevant cost gap could make an unnecessary stop attractive. The signs of costs and rewards matter: maximize rewards and minimize physical time; do not compare a positive bonus to a negative cost without writing the resulting total.

Dense reward is useful when it faithfully decomposes the desired objective, or when its effect on that objective is deliberate. Otherwise it creates a new task. The next reading introduces a shaping form whose return shift can be derived.

Distinguish three operations

Replacing the base reward with a proxy, adding an arbitrary bonus to the base, and adding a potential difference are three different definitions. Log their separate terms. A flag named potential_shaping is not sufficient evidence that the third was implemented.

Recap and self-check

Why does VIA+WAIT’s bonus win?

Because the supplied ad-hoc total is 5, greater than the base-optimal route’s 4. The optimizer follows the specified reward.
