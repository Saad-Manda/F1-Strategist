The Champion Who Keeps Pitting

Phase 5 replaces the table with a neural network. First, watch one that has already been trained.

Below is a Dueling Double DQN, the kind of agent this phase builds. It was trained for 2,000 races on the Pit Wall's 20-lap race by the Phase 5 capstone's learner, and its greedy strategy is 0.28 s from the best possible on average. What you see is its evaluation report: the same network, run the way the real plan's evaluation runs it. Race 29 has no safety car. Hover a lap to see the network's own numbers there, then press Next race a few times and Evaluate 1,000 races.

In race 29 the oracle and the champion both box for B on lap 8. Then the champion boxes again, for A, on lap 9, and finishes 8.53 s behind. Over all races the report puts it 1.94 s from the optimum, seven times its strategy's gap, and 37.8% of races have a stop like that one. Hover lap 9: the network expects 133.32 s to the flag if it stays out and 138.62 s if it boxes.

Case 4 · open

Why does a champion make stops its own network rates worse?

The network is trained, its strategy is within a third of a second of the optimum, and on lap 9 its numbers disagree with the call it made. Is the network wrong about pit stops, did training go wrong, or is something else making the calls? And what should the real plan change?

What changes in Phase 5

Phase 4's learners kept one number per cell of a table, and Phase 4 page 06 showed what that costs: 5-lap cells put the oracle's lap-8 stop in one cell with laps 6, 7 and 9, and no amount of training split them. Phase 5 doesn't fix the binning. It removes the table. A network computes Q(s, a) from the state's numbers, so states that would never share a cell can share what was learned about them. The Bellman target, the TD error and ε-greedy exploration carry over from Phase 4 unchanged.

What Phase 5 does and does not do

This set teaches value-based deep RL as the real plan uses it: a small network in place of the table, the two instabilities of training one online and their fixes (a target network and a replay memory), the three techniques the real agent adds (the dueling architecture, Double DQN and prioritized replay), and the reward-scale trap. It does not cover multi-car states, rivals or self-play, which belong to Phase 6 onward, and it is not a general deep-learning course.
