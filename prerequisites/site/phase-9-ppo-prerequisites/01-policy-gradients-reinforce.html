Policy Gradients From Scratch: REINFORCE

Stop learning values and adjust the policy directly: make the calls that led to good races more likely.

Your call · one race of REINFORCE

The plan's reward: each lap pays −lap time, so the return from any lap to the flag is somewhere between about −11 and −263. REINFORCE moves each call it made by α · Gt · ∇log π(at), with Gt that return.

After one race, what has happened to the calls it made?

A different objective

Phases 4 and 5 learn Q(s, a) and act by its argmax; the policy is a by-product. A policy gradient method makes the policy the thing learned: πθ(a | s), probabilities over the calls with parameters θ, and it climbs the expected return directly.

J(θ) = Eτ ~ πθ[ G(τ) ] ∇θJ(θ) = Eπ[ Σt ∇θ log πθ(at | st) · Gt ]

The second line is the policy gradient theorem, and its point is what is missing from it: the race's dynamics. You need only the policy's own log-probabilities and returns you have sampled.

REINFORCE, one step by hand

Run a race; for every lap, nudge θ along ∇log π(at | st) · Gt. Take two calls with logits zR = 1, zP = 0: π(R) = 0.731, π(P) = 0.269. For a softmax, ∂ log π(a) / ∂za = 1 − π(a) and ∂ log π(a) / ∂zb = −π(b). R was taken and the race returned G = +4; with α = 0.1:

z_R ← 1.0 + 0.1 · 4 · 0.269 = 1.108 z_P ← 0.0 + 0.1 · 4 · (−0.269) = −0.108

The call taken becomes more likely because G was positive. Had G been negative, both moves flip: the call taken becomes less likely. That one step, repeated over every lap of every race, is all of REINFORCE.

Under the raw reward every Gt is negative, so every call made is pushed down, a fast race's a little less than a slow one's. The only learning signal is the difference between races, a few seconds out of about 225, carried on steps sized by the whole 225.

Measured over three seeds, 2,000 races each: REINFORCE ends 20.37 to 42.05 s from the optimum with the raw reward, against 3.46 to 13.23 s with the reward centred (each lap −(lap time − 10)). A uniformly random policy is 43.45 s away. Below, seed 0 both ways.

Even centred, REINFORCE stalls: the entropy reading falls towards zero while the plan is still several seconds off. Its calls stop being random before it has found the good stop, and a call it never makes again can never be learned to be good. Page 02 attacks the noise.

Deep diveThe policy gradient theorem, derived170 words

Write the expectation as a sum over trajectories and use the log-derivative trick, ∇P = P · ∇log P:

∇J = Στ ∇P(τ; θ) · G(τ) = Στ P(τ; θ) · ∇log P(τ; θ) · G(τ) = Eτ[ ∇log P(τ; θ) · G(τ) ]

A trajectory's probability is ρ(s0) · Πt πθ(at | st) · P(st+1 | st, at). Its log is a sum, and only the πθ terms depend on θ: the transition terms' gradients are zero. That leaves E[ Σt ∇log πθ(at | st) · G ]. Replacing G with the return from step t on, Gt, drops terms whose expectation is zero (a call can't change rewards earned before it), which is the form above.

REINFORCE is a Monte Carlo method: it uses the full sampled return, as Phase 3.5's oracle uses the full expected one, rather than Phase 4's one-step bootstrapped target.

Evidence so farWith the raw reward every REINFORCE push is downwards, and the signal is the small difference between races: 20 to 42 s from the optimum, against 3.5 to 13 centred. Next: subtracting a baseline.

Warm-up, in the page · one REINFORCE step by hand

Three actions with logits z = (0.5, 0, −1). The middle action is sampled, the return from that step is G = −3, and the step size is α = 0.2. Give each answer to four decimals (±0.005 is accepted).

The gradient of log π(middle) with respect to the logits is the one-hot vector of the sampled action minus the probabilities.

Now the update z ← z + α · G · ∇ log π(middle).

What did the update do to the action that was taken?
Worked solution for the warm-up

Probabilities 0.5465, 0.3315, 0.1220. Log-gradient of the middle action: (−0.5465, 0.6685, −0.1220), which adds up to 0. With α · G = −0.6 the new logits are (0.8279, −0.4011, −0.9268): the middle action drops from 0.33 to about 0.21.
