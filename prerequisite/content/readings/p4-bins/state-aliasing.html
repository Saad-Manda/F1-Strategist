Test a collision in the complete key

The coarse age rule is min(int((age/50)×10),9). Ages 27 and 29 both fall in bin 5, whose integer ages are 25–29. Age 30 starts bin 6. The old interval descriptions overlapped their endpoints; use half-open bins and handle the normalized maximum separately.

Age collision alone does not prove the whole observation collides: remaining laps and other key fields might distinguish the states. Construct states with the same compound, two laps remaining, weather, temperature, SC flag, usage mask and obligation status, but ages 27 and 29. Different earlier pit histories can produce them at the same current lap. The full coarse keys now agree.

A tiny cliff proves the lost decision

Use an illustrative final-two-lap model. The current lap is charged on the old tyre either way. PIT adds 6 and makes next age 1. The next green lap costs 10 at ages up to 28 and 20 above 28. At current age 27, STAY leads to next age 28 and costs 10 in continuation; PIT costs 6+10=16. STAY wins. At current age 29, STAY leads to age 30 and continuation 20; PIT still costs 16. PIT wins.

The current cost cancels in each branch comparison. The same encoded key now requires conflicting choices. No number of visits can make a single deterministic table policy choose STAY in one physical state and PIT in the other when neither the key nor allowed policy memory separates them. It can only compromise or randomize according to its training mixture.

Choose resolution where actions change

The source's pit-window examples motivate fine age resolution but do not by themselves prove a collision or different optimal actions. Verify complete keys and actual action preferences under your calibrated model. Candidate ages in different bins are not a collision; two within one near-optimal window may still share the same best action.

Using 25–50 age bins gives roughly one- to two-lap resolution over a 50-lap normalization range. Nonuniform bins can concentrate resolution near cliff or pit thresholds. Finer age does not repair missing hidden weather state, or aliasing caused by a very coarse remaining-lap dimension. Check every relevant feature jointly.

Recap and self-check

Can a longer epsilon schedule recover the discarded age distinction?

No. It can gather more evidence for the shared cell, but cannot create a new representational distinction. Change the encoder or the policy's information rather than treating this ceiling as an update bug.
