Lab 01 · Guided build

Who does what in an RL loop?

You’ll build a small environment, connect it to an agent, and investigate what happens when an episode stops before the task is finished.

1. Make a prediction

The world has three positions: 0 → 1 → 2. You start at 0. LEFT moves one position left, bounded at 0; RIGHT moves one right. Arrival at 2 gives reward +5 and ends the task. Every other step gives -1.

Your agent chooses an action. The environment applies it and returns the next observation, reward, and episode boundary flags. Predict what happens after RIGHT, then RIGHT, with a six-step collection limit.

Which result do you expect?

3. Connect the trace to the contract

An agent maps an observation to an action. An environment owns the transition and reward rules. Your action changes the state through env.step(action); the agent doesn’t edit the state directly.

observation, info = env.reset()
action = policy(observation)
observation, reward, terminated, truncated, info = env.step(action)

Reward belongs to one transition. The sum in the trace is this episode’s undiscounted return. Arrival at the goal is a true task boundary: terminated=True. An external cap ends collection early: truncated=True. Stop the agent loop on either flag, then explicitly reset before a new episode.

The base chain has no time limit. Its collector can be wrapped with a cap. Later, value learners will still bootstrap at a nonterminal truncation; they won’t at true termination. An intrinsic finite-horizon task is different: reaching its horizon is termination, and remaining time belongs in its state.

info is diagnostic data; here it records the number of executed steps. Your policy uses the observation. A fresh reset clears episode counters and gives an initial observation without taking an action or awarding a step reward.

Help, when you want it

Hint 1: Compare what the reset call returns with what a step returns. What does each belong to: agent or environment?

Hint 2: The terminal observation belongs to the episode that just ended. Reset is a separate call. The wrapper can stop collection while the chain’s position is still 1.

Hint 3: Return (position, reward, terminated, False, info) from the base chain. The collector must break when terminated or truncated is true. Reset all episode counters.

Explained solution

Reset initializes position 0 and clears counters. A step validates the action, computes the next position, then assigns its reward and true-terminal flag. The TimeLimit wrapper supplies truncation. The collector records the transition before checking both boundary flags:

observation, info = env.reset(seed=seed)
trace = []
while True:
    before = observation
    action = policy(observation)
    observation, reward, terminated, truncated, info = env.step(action)
    trace.append(dict(before=before, action=action,
                      observation=observation, reward=reward,
                      terminated=terminated, truncated=truncated, info=info))
    if terminated or truncated:
        break

RIGHT,RIGHT yields rewards -1 and +5, total +4, and terminal observation 2. A cap of 1 yields observation 1, reward -1, and truncation. The broken collector checks only termination and steps after the cap.
