A policy can keep choosing the only thing it knows

Suppose a one-step world has actions SMALL, ending for reward 1, and LARGE, ending for reward 5. Both table entries start at zero. A first-action tie rule chooses SMALL. With alpha 0.5 its value becomes 0.5, larger than LARGE's unvisited zero. A purely greedy policy now keeps choosing SMALL; its estimate approaches 1 while LARGE remains zero forever.

The best action was executable and legal, but never discovered. More episodes of the same behavior do not provide the missing samples. Conversely, in negative-reward tasks zero initialization is optimistic, so untouched actions can become preferable after known actions turn negative. That can encourage some exploration, but it is not a universal coverage guarantee.

Exploration trades current quality for information

Exploration chooses actions partly to obtain evidence. Exploitation uses current estimates to choose what looks best. An exploratory action can cost time now and improve later decisions; it can also simply be costly. The task, training budget and sample coverage determine whether the tradeoff pays.

A random policy is useful for exposing branches and bugs, but uniform action randomness does not guarantee visiting every reachable state often. Some states require long action sequences. An environment can also contain unreachable states, so “every rectangular table cell visited” is not a reasonable requirement without reachability analysis.

Explore within the permitted set

If the stored value 99 belongs to an illegal action, it is excluded during both exploitation and exploration. A mask is a known constraint, not another uncertain action to discover by violating it. Every nonterminal decision must retain at least one legal option; report a contract error when none exists.

Ties affect discovery too. Uniform tie-breaking lets every equally valued legal action have a chance. First-index tie-breaking is reproducible but may bias early choices. Declare the convention before comparing algorithms.

Recap and self-check

Can the SMALL-only learner infer LARGE=5 from repeatedly receiving SMALL's reward 1?

No. Those observations contain no evidence of LARGE's outcome. It needs relevant experience or an available model, rather than simply more of the same updates.
