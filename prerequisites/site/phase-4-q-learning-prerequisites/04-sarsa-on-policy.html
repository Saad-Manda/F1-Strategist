On-Policy vs. Off-Policy: SARSA

Change one term in Q-learning's target and the learner starts to price in its own clumsiness.

Your call · push or pit

A learner in training pushes one more lap on old tyres. On the next lap it explores and pushes again, into the cliff: −30. A Q-learner and a SARSA learner both update their value for that first push.

Which one marks the push down further?

SARSA's update

Q(s, a) ← Q(s, a) + α [ r + γ · Q(s′, a′) − Q(s, a) ]
Target
Q-learningr + γ · max_a′ Q(s′, a′)
SARSAr + γ · Q(s′, a′), where a′ is the call actually made next

Q-learning asks "what is the best I could do from s′?" SARSA asks "what will I do from s′, given that I still explore?" Its a′ is the call its own ε-greedy policy picks, chosen before this step's update is computed. The name is the five things the update uses: State, Action, Reward, State, Action.

That is what on-policy means: the target is built from the policy that is making the data, exploring included. SARSA's values carry the real chance that its own exploring will stumble into something costly. Q-learning's never do.

Work it: push or pit, side by side

s1, PIT_NOW → r = −12, terminal safe: a known cost s1, PUSH → r = −1, next = s2 cheap now, risk later s2, PIT → r = −8, terminal the clean recovery s2, PUSH_MORE → r = −30, terminal the cliff

Played perfectly, PUSH then PIT costs −9 and beats PIT_NOW's −12. But a learner in training sometimes explores into PUSH_MORE at s2. Both learners, α = 0.5, γ = 1, tables at 0:

The reveal · after episode 3

The numbers saySARSA: −8.375 against −2.875

Q-learning's target assumes the recovery always follows, even on the episode where it didn't, so the close call barely registers. SARSA's target is built from what happened next, exploring and all.

Over thousands of episodes the pattern holds: Q-learning heads for the bold policy that is best if played perfectly, SARSA for a more cautious one that allows for its own mistakes while it still explores.

Why the plan trains both

Neither is more correct. They answer two questions: "what is best if I play perfectly?" and "what is best for an agent that still explores?" So the real Definition of Done trains both on the same races and compares them. Q-learning is expected to find the optimal policy faster; SARSA something a little more conservative. The gap between them is the lesson, not a spare experiment.

Deep diveThe pre-commit, and the leak it allows100 words

SARSA needs a′ before it can finish this step's update, but a training loop asks for the next call only after the update. So the learner chooses a′ inside the update, keeps it, and hands it out at the next call. If a race stops before that next call comes (a step limit, a red flag, an exception), the kept call is still waiting when the next race starts, and that race's first move was chosen for a different situation. Page 07's capstone builds the mechanism and catches the leak; page 08 finds the same gap in the plan's SARSAAgent.

Evidence so farSARSA prices its own exploring; Q-learning assumes the best follows. Both learn by bootstrapping from the next state's cell. Next: what a cell is, when the state is not a short list of counts.
