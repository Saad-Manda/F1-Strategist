Why this matters

A good decision must account for all its possible consequences. An expectation is an average weighted by their probabilities. It describes the model's average before an outcome is known, rather than the most likely outcome or the best possible one.

Calculate the contributions

A route produces reward 5 with probability 0.8 and reward −5 with probability 0.2. Multiply each reward by its probability, then add: 0.8 × 5 + 0.2 × (−5) = 3. A safe route always gives 2. Under an expected-reward objective the first route is better, although it sometimes performs worse than the safe one.

The expected immediate reward is 0.8 × 2 + 0.2 × (−1) = 1.4. The expected continuation is 0.8 × 4 + 0.2 × 1 = 3.4. With gamma 0.5, the expected combined score is 1.4 + 0.5 × 3.4 = 3.1.

Equivalently, score each outcome first: prompt gives 2 + 0.5 × 4 = 4; delayed gives −1 + 0.5 × 1 = −0.5. Their weighted average is 0.8 × 4 + 0.2 × (−0.5) = 3.1. This equality is linearity of expectation: weighted sums can be averaged term by term. It does not require reward and continuation to be independent.

Read the notation and code

E[X] = Σ_i p_i x_i means: for each outcome i, multiply its probability p_i by the quantity x_i being scored, then sum. E denotes expectation. The notation does not mean “take the largest x.”

def expected_value(probabilities, values):
    validate_distribution(probabilities)
    if len(probabilities) != len(values):
        raise ValueError("one value per outcome required")
    return sum(p * value for p, value in zip(probabilities, values))

assert abs(expected_value([0.8, 0.2], [5, -5]) - 3) < 1e-10

validate_distribution is the function from the previous reading. zip pairs each probability with its corresponding value. The length check matters because zip would otherwise silently stop at the shorter list.

Samples and rare outcomes

Four observed rewards [5, 5, 5, −5] average to 2.5. That sample average differs from the exact model expectation 3. More representative independent samples can improve an estimate, but a short sample does not change the specified probability row.

A rare bad outcome still matters. Reward +1 with probability 0.99 and reward −200 with probability 0.01 has expectation 0.99 − 2 = −1.01. A few runs may never encounter the bad case. Maximizing expectation also does not guarantee acceptable worst-case risk; risk limits would be an additional modeling requirement.

Common mistake: choosing the best successor after observing the random outcome in an offline calculation when the actual agent must choose before that outcome is known.

Does the reward-3 expectation mean that a sampled reward must equal 3?

No. The sampled rewards in that model are 5 or −5. Three is their probability-weighted average.
