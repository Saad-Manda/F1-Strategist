Phase 3.5 · Case brief

Beating the oracle

Phase 3.5 builds the oracle: the best possible strategy for the race, computed, not learned. First, beat it.

Race #257 is the Pit Wall's 20-lap race from Phase 1, with the oracle switched on. After every call it tells you its own call and what yours cost, on average. Play it this way: stay out on A until the safety car comes, then box for B. If no safety car has come by lap 12, box anyway.

Played that way, race #257 ends at 227.76 s. The oracle, with the same safety car on the same laps, finishes at 230.12 s. You beat the optimal strategy by 2.36 s, and on laps 8 and 9 it told you that you were making mistakes.

How did you beat the optimal strategy?

The oracle is worked out from the rules of the race, every situation, with nothing left to chance in the arithmetic. Nothing can be better. Yet you beat it, with calls it marked as wrong. Is the oracle wrong? Is "optimal" the wrong word? And when a learning agent beats it in Phase 4 or 5, what should you conclude?

You will collect the evidence one page at a time:

  1. Planning: where the oracle's calls come from, without a single race played.
  2. Backward induction: how it prices lap 8 by starting at the flag.
  3. Expectations: what "best" means when nobody knows the dice. The maths of the case closes here.
  4. Markov chains: a second oracle that knows the weather, and how far it beats the first.
  5. The build: you write the solver the Pit Wall runs, stage by stage.
  6. Back to the plan: how the real project scores agents against the oracle, and three places its tests can mislead. The case closes.

Why build an oracle at all?

Phases 4, 5 and 9 train agents that learn from experience. Without an oracle, the strongest claim they allow is "my agent beat the rule I wrote", and that rule's weaknesses are the ones you thought of. With one, the plan's headline becomes "my RL agent came within 0.4 s of provable optimum", with the laps where it loses the time. The oracle can give that number because this race is small enough to solve outright, and solving it is planning, not learning.

What Phase 3.5 does and does not do

Phase 3.5 builds an evaluator, not an agent. So this set teaches two things in depth: backward induction, the algorithm that solves a race of fixed length, and Markov chains, the weather model the oracle needs. Q-learning, TD updates, exploration and neural networks are not covered, even in preview. They belong to Phases 4 and 5.
