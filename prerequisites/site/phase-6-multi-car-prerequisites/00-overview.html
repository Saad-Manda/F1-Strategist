Phase 6 · Case brief

The Undercut That Doesn't Exist

Phase 6 puts other cars on the track. First, race one.

The Pit Wall now has a rival. It is 0.3 s a lap slower than you, starts 0.3 s ahead, and stops once for B on lap 9, whatever you do. This is the race the real plan builds, with one rule the plan keeps: each car drives its own lap time, and position is whoever has used less time. Try the classic move. Stay out to lap 6, box for B three laps before the rival, and come out ahead when it stops. Then race it again with a stop on lap 8. Then open the board under the race and change the lap the rival stops on.

The undercut works, in the sense that you finish ahead: 226.07 s against the rival's 231.37 s. But a stop on lap 8 is 1.28 s quicker, and the board shows why that matters: lap 8 is the best stop when the rival stops on lap 5, on lap 9 or on lap 14. The rival's stop never moves yours. Every race time on this page counts the 0.3 s you start behind.

Case 5 · open

Why does stopping before the rival never gain anything?

In real racing the undercut is the most-timed call in a pit stop: stop a lap or two before the car you are fighting, use the fresh tyres while it is still on old ones, and come out in front. In the plan's race, stopping before the rival is worth nothing that stopping on your own best lap doesn't give you anyway. Is the toy race too small to show it, is the reward missing something, or is something missing from the race? And what should the real plan change?

What changes in Phase 6

Through Phase 5 the agent raced alone, and the reward, minus the lap time, was the race. Phase 6 adds rivals that follow fixed pit plans, three new numbers in the state about the cars around you, rewards that care about finishing ahead, and two small models of racing: losing time in another car's wake, and passing. None of it changes how your own tyres wear. All of it is about outcomes that depend on cars you don't control.
